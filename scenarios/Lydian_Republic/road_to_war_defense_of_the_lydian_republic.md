# Road to War: Defense of the Lydian Republic

## Fictional Operational-Level Wargame Background

> **Scenario status:** Entirely fictional. The Republic of Lydia, the Federation of Vostia, the Regional Defense Coalition, their political organizations, military forces, geographic locations, and the events described below are invented for research and educational use.

| Scenario parameter | Date or condition |
| --- | --- |
| Road-to-war period | 2026 – May 2030 |
| Vostian invasion begins | 16 May 2030, 0600 local time |
| Scenario begins | 18 May 2030, 0600 local time (H+48) |
| Game scale | Operational; brigade-sized maneuver formations; 12-hour turns |

## 1. Strategic Background

The Republic of Lydia is a small parliamentary democracy on the frontier of the Federation of Vostia. Since the mid-2010s, successive Lydian governments have pursued closer economic and security ties with neighboring democratic states and the **Regional Defense Coalition (RDC)**. Lydia is an RDC partner but not a formal member. By 2026, it participates in coalition-sponsored military education, disaster-response exercises, and small-unit training. Most Lydians support closer regional integration, although the population remains divided over its pace and military implications.

The RDC is an association of states created to coordinate regional security, military access, and collective crisis response. It does not maintain a large standing army. Instead, member states provide forces to coalition headquarters for exercises and contingencies. This arrangement gives the RDC significant potential combat power but requires political consultation and time to assemble, deploy, and sustain a multinational force.

Vostia accepts Lydia's formal independence but insists that it remain militarily neutral and economically tied to Vostia. Vostian leaders regard Lydia as part of a historically vital sphere of influence and portray expanding RDC access, exercises, and defense relationships as an attempt to encircle Vostia. They also fear the political example of a neighboring state leaving Vostia's orbit and prospering under coalition protection.

Eastern Lydia is central to the dispute. Ethnic Vostians form a substantial minority in and around Kirov and Aradesh. Although most participate peacefully in Lydian political life, economic stagnation, language-policy disputes, and perceptions of neglect provide fertile ground for agitation. Vostian media amplify genuine grievances, portray routine law-enforcement actions as ethnic persecution, and support organizations advocating neutrality, autonomy, or union with Vostia. Over time, the distinction between legitimate opposition groups, Vostian-funded activists, and covertly organized separatist cells becomes increasingly difficult to determine.

Lydia also has strategic importance beyond its size. Its highways and railways connect Vostia with regional markets, while the Arel River corridor, energy pipelines, and electrical interconnections support trade across the region. Kirov is eastern Lydia's principal transportation hub, and Aradesh is its administrative and political center. Control of the Arel River crossings at Kirov, Belas, and Velin governs movement between eastern and central Lydia.

The opposing military strategies are built around time. Lydia cannot independently defeat a large Vostian attack and must hold key terrain until coalition reinforcements arrive. The RDC seeks to deter aggression without permanently stationing a large force in Lydia, relying instead on exercises, rotational deployments, prepositioned equipment, and rapid reinforcement. Vostia can concentrate combat power near the border faster than the coalition can authorize, deploy, and fully integrate the planned corps-sized response force. If war comes, Vostia intends to seize Aradesh, Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, and the principal Arel crossings near Belas and Velin before the military balance shifts.

## 2. Political Competition and Hybrid Pressure, 2026–2029

### 2026–2027: Relations Deteriorate

In late 2026, Lydia joins a regional initiative to modernize the Arel River rail corridor and connect its electrical grid more closely with neighboring states. Vostia responds with customs delays, energy pressure, and an information campaign alleging that coalition engineers are surveying sites for permanent bases and long-range weapons. Lydian journalists also uncover concealed Vostian-linked financing for the Lydian Renewal Front, a legal party advocating military neutrality and greater autonomy for the eastern provinces.

During 2027, pressure expands below the threshold of armed conflict. Reductions in Vostian natural-gas deliveries raise prices and disrupt eastern Lydian industry, while a cyberattack interrupts rail scheduling and government services. Lydia accelerates economic diversification and establishes an annual resilience and infrastructure-protection exercise with the RDC. Vostia depicts the limited exercise as preparation to deploy a coalition division.

Vostia also conducts an unannounced readiness exercise opposite the Drovna Heights and Aradesh Plain, leaving some forces near the border after the exercise ends. It then simplifies citizenship procedures for ethnic Vostians abroad. Thousands of eastern Lydians obtain Vostian passports, creating a population Vostian officials later claim a responsibility to protect.

### 2028: The Contest Moves Inside Lydia

A centrist coalition wins Lydia's January 2028 election on a platform of regional economic integration, energy diversification, and stronger RDC defense ties. Vostian trade restrictions deepen unemployment in eastern industrial districts, while Vostian-backed media blame the decline on Lydia's external alignment. Disputes over a Lydian-language administrative reform trigger large demonstrations in Kirov and Aradesh. Most remain peaceful, but small groups attack police stations and municipal offices.

By midyear, the crisis begins to acquire an armed dimension. Lydian security forces discover military-grade weapons, explosives, and encrypted radios near routes through the Drovna Heights. Explosions later damage infrastructure near Kirov and Aradesh. A previously unknown organization—the Eastern Lydian Protection Movement—claims responsibility and demands regional autonomy, although its public leadership denies directing violence.

In November, Lydia and the RDC announce negotiations for an agreement covering exercises, rotational deployments, prepositioned equipment, logistics access, and mutual consultation during a crisis. The proposal does not authorize permanent coalition bases. Vostia offers economic concessions in exchange for permanent Lydian neutrality and a prohibition on foreign forces. Lydia rejects the proposal as an infringement on its sovereignty.

### 2029: Deterrence Creates a Security Dilemma

Vostia opens 2029 with its largest frontier military exercise in several years. Armored and mechanized formations rehearse rapid deployment toward Lydia, and bridging equipment, communications systems, and supplies remain forward after most maneuver units return to garrison. In April, malicious software causes rolling electrical outages across eastern Lydia. Lydia attributes the attack to Vostian military intelligence; Vostia denies involvement.

On 22 June, Lydia and the RDC sign the Defense Cooperation and Access Agreement. The agreement requires political consultation and assistance in response to armed aggression and permits rotational coalition deployments, access to training areas, and prepositioning of equipment. It does not grant Lydia full RDC membership, establish permanent coalition bases, or authorize attacks from Lydia into Vostia. Despite Vostian economic pressure, cyberattacks, protests, and forged allegations of secret missile provisions, the Lydian parliament ratifies the agreement on 14 September. It enters into force on 1 October, and small coalition logistics and site-preparation teams begin receiving equipment west of the Arel River.

By the end of 2029, activists in Kirov and Aradesh establish “people's councils” claiming to represent eastern Lydia. Armed self-defense groups guard their offices and patrol at night, while Vostian media treat the councils as legitimate regional authorities. Exchanges of fire at isolated border crossings further increase tensions. Lydia reinforces its border posts, and Vostia establishes a restricted military zone on its side of the frontier.

## 3. The 2030 Crisis

### January–March: Preparations Hidden by Exercises

On 8 January, Lydia and the RDC announce **Lydian Shield 30**, a major May exercise built around the **RDC Coalition Land Corps**. The planned force includes the **1st Multinational Armored Division** with one armored brigade, two mechanized brigades, and a division artillery group; the **2nd Multinational Infantry Division** with one mechanized brigade, two light infantry brigades, and a division artillery group; and the **7th Coalition Armored Brigade** under corps control. Participating formations come from several RDC member states, arrive in echelons, and use a combination of deployed and prepositioned equipment while training under a common coalition command. The exercise is defensive, yet Vostia presents it as proof that a permanent foreign military presence is emerging in Lydia.

Vostia responds with **Union Resolve 30**, a larger readiness exercise in its frontier military district. Unlike earlier drills, transportation units, field hospitals, air defenses, ammunition, fuel, and bridging equipment deploy forward with the maneuver formations. The exercise gives Vostia both political cover and the logistical posture needed for an invasion.

Meanwhile, the Eastern Lydian Protection Movement organizes strikes and roadblocks in Kirov and Aradesh. False reports of mass arrests spread online, while sabotage derails a train carrying coalition engineering equipment and closes a key rail line for nine days. On 21 March, cyberattacks disrupt cellular service, emergency dispatch, and parts of the electrical grid as anonymous warnings of impending government repression bring larger crowds into the streets.

### April: The Military Balance Shifts

**2–10 April — Separatists establish local control.** Protesters occupy public squares in Kirov and Aradesh, and armed individuals appear among otherwise peaceful crowds. Police withdraw from parts of Kirov after gunfire near a government building. The people's councils establish Eastern Protection Committees, erect checkpoints, and openly display military weapons. Lydia declares the groups illegal but avoids a general assault that might cause civilian casualties or provide Vostia with a pretext to intervene.

**14 April — Lydia partially mobilizes.** Selected army and national-guard formations deploy to protect the capital, airfields, government facilities, and the Arel River crossings. Because many units are committed to infrastructure, internal security, and rear-area protection, Lydia cannot form a dense defense along the Vostian border.

**20 April — Vostian forces move forward.** Under cover of Union Resolve 30, the **Vostian 3rd Field Army** moves the 7th and 12th Maneuver Corps into forward assembly areas within striking distance of Lydia. Their four armored and mechanized divisions orient generally toward the northern routes, Aradesh Plain, and Maren approaches, while both corps artillery groups occupy supporting areas. Vostia describes the movement as a routine exercise phase and refuses requests for foreign observers.

**28–30 April — Coalition deployment accelerates and diplomacy fails.** The RDC advances the opening of Lydian Shield 30. The Coalition Land Corps headquarters and both multinational division headquarters establish command and support nodes. Mechanized elements disperse toward Kirov, Aradesh, and Belas; light infantry elements move toward the Drovna Heights and Maren–Velin approaches; both division artillery groups occupy initial support areas; and armored elements begin drawing or completing issue of prepositioned equipment west of the Arel River. The arriving formations are not yet fully concentrated or integrated. Emergency talks fail when Vostia demands cancellation of the defense agreement, withdrawal of coalition forces, eastern autonomy, and a permanent ban on foreign troops. Lydia accepts international monitoring and renewed governance talks but refuses limits on its external relationships.

### May: From Political Crisis to Invasion

**7 May — Aradesh shooting.** Gunfire during rival demonstrations near the provincial assembly kills twelve protesters and police. Edited videos circulate before investigators determine who fired first. Vostia calls the incident a massacre; Lydia alleges deliberate provocation.

**11 May, 2140 — Separatist-office explosion.** An explosion destroys the Eastern Lydian Protection Movement's political office in Kirov, killing several prominent activists. Vostia immediately blames Lydian security services. Lydia denies involvement and calls the blast a provocation. Armed separatists occupy the site before independent investigators can examine it, leaving responsibility unresolved.

**12 May — Government buildings seized.** Armed separatists occupy provincial offices, police facilities, radio stations, and transportation-control centers in parts of Kirov and Aradesh. They declare provisional administrations and request Vostian protection. Lydian security forces retain other parts of both cities, producing fragmented control and sporadic fighting.

**13–14 May — Ultimatum and recognition.** Vostia gives Lydia twenty-four hours to halt security operations, withdraw from Kirov and Aradesh, and move all coalition combat forces west of the Arel River. Lydia rejects the demands but again offers to accept international observers. Vostia then recognizes the self-declared **Kirov People's Republic** and **Aradesh People's Republic**. Both sign security-assistance treaties with Vostia and request immediate military support.

**15 May — Final preparations.** Union Resolve 30 shifts without announcement from exercise procedures to combat deployment. Vostian artillery occupies firing positions; electronic warfare disrupts Lydian communications; and cyberattacks affect rail dispatch, fuel distribution, and government networks. Separatist groups mark routes, block selected Lydian movements, and report defensive positions. Vostia claims Lydian forces are preparing to attack the newly recognized republics. All coalition formations represented in the base scenario are now in Lydia, but several have only recently completed movement or equipment issue and the corps has not completed its planned exercise concentration. Additional RDC forces remain outside the theater and will not arrive as playable formations during the immediate campaign.

## 4. Invasion and Transition to Play

### 16 May 2030: D-Day

**0600 — Vostian forces cross the border.** Vostia announces a “limited peace-enforcement operation” to protect the two eastern republics, remove the threat posed by foreign forces, and restore regional stability. The Vostian 3rd Field Army crosses on multiple axes:

- The **12th Maneuver Corps**, the field army main effort, advances across the Aradesh Plain toward Aradesh while developing the southern approach through the Maren Lowlands.
- The **7th Maneuver Corps**, the supporting effort, advances through the northern Aradesh Plain and Drovna Heights toward Kirov and the Northwest Bridge approaches.
- Armored formations drive along the open approaches, mechanized and infantry formations develop the restrictive northern and southern sectors, and the two corps artillery groups support the advance.

Vostian long-range fires, electronic warfare, cyber operations, and separatist activity disrupt Lydian command and movement. Dispersed Lydian border forces delay the advance but cannot hold their positions.

**0830 — Lydia requests coalition assistance.** The Lydian government invokes the Defense Cooperation and Access Agreement. After emergency consultation, the RDC Council authorizes coalition forces already in theater to defend internationally recognized Lydian territory. The authorization does not permit a general invasion of Vostia. Member states also begin preparing follow-on forces, but national approval procedures and deployment distances delay their arrival.

**Afternoon — The coalition exercise becomes a combat operation.** The RDC Coalition Land Corps assumes responsibility for the ground defense, exercising operational control over both multinational divisions and the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade. Units already participating in or preparing for Lydian Shield 30 form the initial defensive force. The sudden transition leaves the corps dispersed and only partially integrated, dependent on transportation and communications networks already degraded by sabotage and cyberattack. Differences in national procedures and communications systems add friction, although the formations have previously trained under coalition command.

### 17 May 2030: The Defense Takes Shape

Vostian leading elements penetrate the border zone and advance toward Kirov and Aradesh. Follow-on formations move through breached border routes while other units secure supply lines and support the northern and southern flanks. The 12th Maneuver Corps remains oriented on the central and southern approaches as the field army main effort; the 7th Maneuver Corps maintains pressure in the northern sector. Neither corps has yet seized its decisive cities or an Arel crossing.

The **1st Multinational Armored Division** begins establishing its area defense in the Aradesh–Belas sector. Its mechanized brigades cover the essential terrain and routes while the 1st Coalition Armored Brigade prepares to serve as the division's mobile reserve. The **2nd Multinational Infantry Division** begins establishing its area defense across Kirov, the Drovna Heights, and the Maren–Velin approaches with its mechanized and light infantry brigades. Both division artillery groups move into supporting positions. West of the Arel River, the **7th Coalition Armored Brigade** completes its accelerated equipment draw and concentration as the corps reserve, positioned to reinforce either division or counterattack on corps order.

Lydian forces protect rear areas, airfields, government facilities, and portions of the Arel crossings while managing displaced civilians and fighting separatists. They are not represented as playable maneuver counters in the base scenario. Coalition and Lydian leaders recognize that the loss of Kirov and Aradesh would isolate eastern Lydia, discredit the defense agreement, and open the approaches to the Arel River.

### 18 May 2030, 0600: Scenario Start (H+48)

The first forty-eight hours of the invasion are pre-scenario penetration, deployment, and command-transition events. The fixed Blue and Red operations orders—and their four overlapping, conditions-based phases—take effect at this point.

At the start of play:

- The Vostian 3rd Field Army's 7th and 12th Maneuver Corps have broken through the Lydian border zone but have not captured Kirov, Aradesh, or an Arel crossing.
- The 12th Maneuver Corps is the field army main effort, oriented on Aradesh and the central–southern objective system; the 7th Maneuver Corps is the supporting effort in the Kirov–Drovna sector.
- Vostia retains local numerical superiority, operational momentum, and the initiative.
- The RDC Coalition Land Corps controls the 1st Multinational Armored Division, the 2nd Multinational Infantry Division, and the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade.
- The 1st Multinational Armored Division defends the Aradesh–Belas sector; the 2nd Multinational Infantry Division defends Kirov and blocks the Drovna and Maren–Velin approaches.
- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains under corps control as the mobile reserve and may reinforce, block, or counterattack only on corps order.
- Both coalition division artillery groups are available in general support of their parent divisions; their positions and phase-specific support priorities remain command decisions.
- The Northwest Bridge and the Arel crossings associated with Kirov, Belas, and Velin remain under Lydian or coalition control and usable. They are critical to reinforcement, lateral movement, and continuation of the defense.
- Additional coalition forces are moving toward the theater but will not arrive in time to solve the immediate operational problem.
- Coalition forces may operate throughout Lydia but may not conduct a general attack into Vostia.

The coalition commander must determine how much forward space can be exchanged for time without surrendering Aradesh, Kirov, or an Arel crossing; identify the Vostian main effort; and decide when and where to commit the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade. The Vostian commander must execute a fixed operational design in which the 12th Maneuver Corps remains the field army main effort and seizes Aradesh, Belas, and Velin in sequence while the 7th Maneuver Corps secures Kirov and the Northwest Bridge. The principal Red decisions concern tempo, concentration of fires, protection of the corps seam and flanks, and preservation or commitment of reserves within that design.

## 5. Matters Intentionally Left Unresolved

The shared road-to-war does not resolve several questions that may be treated differently in side-specific intelligence products or scenario-control instructions:

- Who ordered the 11 May explosion at the separatist office.
- How much control separatist political leaders exercise over armed groups acting in their name.
- Whether Vostia decided on invasion months in advance or retained a genuine diplomatic off-ramp until the final week.
- How unified RDC member governments will remain if the campaign expands or becomes prolonged.
- The precise arrival schedule, national composition, and capabilities of follow-on coalition forces.

These unresolved questions preserve narrative uncertainty and provide opportunities for later scenario injects without changing the basic operational situation.

## 6. Design Note

The actors and events in this road-to-war are fictional. The scenario draws on general patterns of interstate competition, hybrid coercion, separatist conflict, multinational crisis response, prepositioned equipment, and the operational challenge of reinforcing an exposed partner before an aggressor can achieve a territorial fait accompli.
