## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The corps area of interest includes eastern Lydia from the Vostian frontier west through the Arel River belt and the approaches to central Lydia. It includes the northern routes through the Drovna Heights, the Kirov transportation hub, the Aradesh Plain, the Maren Lowlands, and the east-west routes connecting these areas with the crossings near Kirov, Belas, and Velin.

Events beyond the immediate area that may affect the operation include the movement of additional Vostian formations toward Lydia; delayed arrival of additional RDC forces; Vostian political efforts to gain recognition for separatist administrations; and cyber, electronic, or proxy activity against Lydian transportation, communications, and government systems.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

- **Aradesh Plain:** Open terrain favors armored and mechanized movement and permits Vostia to mass and shift combat power. Aradesh is the political center of eastern Lydia and the initial focus of the corps main effort.
- **Kirov transportation hub:** Kirov controls important east-west and north-south routes. Its loss would disrupt lateral movement and threaten to isolate the northern sector.
- **Drovna Heights:** Rough, forested terrain restricts movement and observation, favors infantry defense, and provides routes for a northern turning movement if left uncovered.
- **Maren Lowlands:** Marshes and drainage features canalize movement onto a limited number of firm routes. These routes can support a southern envelopment but also create opportunities for blocking positions.
- **Arel River belt:** The Arel River is the last major natural obstacle before central Lydia. The crossings near Kirov, Belas, and Velin are essential to coalition reinforcement, lateral movement, and continued defense. Vostian control of an intact crossing would threaten the corps operational depth.
- **Road network:** Two principal east-west routes and several secondary roads connect the sectors. Control of junctions and lateral routes is as important as control of individual positions because the corps must move scarce mobile formations between separated threats.

### c. Enemy Forces

#### (1) Composition and Disposition

The Vostian 3rd Field Army controls two maneuver corps with four divisions and corps artillery. Corps intelligence assesses:

- the **7th Maneuver Corps**, containing the 11th Armored Division, 14th Mechanized Division, and 7th Corps Artillery Group, is oriented generally toward Kirov and the northern/central routes;
- the **12th Maneuver Corps**, containing the 18th Armored Division, 22nd Mechanized Division, and 12th Corps Artillery Group, is oriented generally toward Aradesh, the central plain, and southern approaches; and
- infantry forces will support operations in the Drovna Heights and Maren Lowlands while mechanized and armored formations attack along the principal routes.

Vostian leading forces have penetrated the former Lydian border zone but have not captured Kirov or Aradesh. Follow-on forces retain operational momentum and local numerical superiority. The field army main effort remains the subject of continuing coalition intelligence assessment.

#### (2) Capabilities and Limitations

Vostian forces can mass armored, mechanized, and artillery combat power against one sector; attack Kirov and Aradesh simultaneously; turn the corps flanks through restrictive terrain; exploit an exposed route toward the Arel River; and use cyber, electronic warfare, and separatist activity to increase friction.

Vostia is constrained by a requirement for rapid political and territorial gains, extending lines of communication, the need to secure occupied cities and routes, restrictive northern and southern terrain, and the risk that premature dispersion will expose a corps or division to coalition counterattack.

#### (3) Most Likely Course of Action

Vostia conducts simultaneous attacks toward Aradesh and Kirov while applying supporting pressure through the Drovna Heights and Maren Lowlands. It uses mechanized forces to fix coalition defenders at the cities, armored forces to penetrate or bypass the central defense, and infantry to protect the field army flanks and routes. Once a city or sector is isolated, Vostia exploits toward the nearest usable Arel crossing and commits follow-on forces to secure the gain.

#### (4) Most Dangerous Course of Action

Vostia concentrates the majority of its armored combat power on one narrow axis, achieves a rapid penetration before the coalition can identify the main effort, bypasses a city rather than becoming fixed in urban combat, and seizes an Arel crossing while supporting attacks prevent either division or the corps reserve from responding. This course could divide the corps and open the approaches to central Lydia.

### d. Friendly Forces

#### (1) Higher Headquarters Mission and Intent

The RDC Council has authorized coalition forces in Lydia to assist the internationally recognized Lydian government under the Defense Cooperation and Access Agreement. The coalition must halt the Vostian attack, retain the principal cities and transportation network, preserve Lydian sovereignty, and create conditions for restoration of territorial integrity.

The purpose of RDC military action is to deny Vostia a rapid territorial fait accompli while preserving coalition credibility and political cohesion. The desired end state is a viable Lydian government, a halted Vostian offensive, key transportation and river terrain denied to Vostia, and an RDC force capable of continuing operations as follow-on political and military options develop.

#### (2) Lydian Forces and Agencies

Lydian military and security forces protect rear areas, government facilities, airfields, critical infrastructure, displaced civilians, and portions of the Arel crossings. They provide local information, maintain civil authority, and coordinate with RDC formations through established liaison channels. Their support does not relieve RDC formations of assigned terrain responsibilities.

#### (3) Adjacent and Supporting Forces

Additional RDC formations are moving toward the theater but will not arrive in time to resolve the immediate 10-day operational problem. Coalition airpower, attack aviation, reconnaissance, engineering, air defense, electronic warfare, logistics, and medical capabilities are integrated within parent formations or coordinated by higher headquarters.

### e. Civil Considerations

Kirov and Aradesh contain divided populations, displaced civilians, government facilities, transportation nodes, and critical infrastructure. Separatist activity and fragmented local control may complicate movement and identification. Commanders will preserve civilian life and essential infrastructure consistent with mission accomplishment, maintain access for Lydian emergency services where possible, and avoid actions that undermine the coalition's defensive mandate.

### f. Attachments and Detachments

None at H-hour. Cross-attachment of brigades requires corps approval. The base task organization remains authoritative unless modified by fragmentary order.

### g. Assumptions

- No additional RDC maneuver formation arrives during the 10-day operation unless directed by higher headquarters.
- The Arel crossings begin usable; changes to bridge status require confirmation by the responsible engineer element or higher headquarters.
- Coalition forces retain freedom to operate throughout Lydia but not to conduct a general attack into Vostia.
- Lydian forces continue rear-area and civil-security functions unless a higher-headquarters direction states otherwise.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local time, the **RDC Coalition Land Corps conducts an area defense, organized in depth, in eastern Lydia to retain Aradesh and Kirov, deny Vostian use of the Arel River crossings, defeat or contain operational penetrations, and preserve conditions for coalition reinforcement and restoration of Lydian territorial integrity.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander's Intent

#### Purpose

Deny Vostia a rapid territorial fait accompli and preserve the coalition's ability to continue the defense of Lydia after the initial 10-day campaign.

#### Key Tasks

- Retain Aradesh and deny Vostia political control of eastern Lydia.
- Retain Kirov and the routes connecting northern, central, and western Lydia.
- Deny Vostian seizure or use of the Arel River crossings.
- Prevent northern or southern maneuver from unhinging the central defense.
- Identify the Vostian main effort before committing the corps reserve.
- Defeat or contain operationally significant penetrations.
- Preserve enough coalition combat power to remain effective through 28 May 2030, 0600 local.
- Protect critical civilian infrastructure and comply with restrictions on operations outside Lydia.

- **Friendly:** The corps remains coherent; both divisions can continue operations; and a mobile force remains available for renewed defense or follow-on operations.
- **Terrain:** Aradesh and Kirov remain under coalition/Lydian control. Vostia controls no usable Arel crossing or unopposed route into central Lydia.
- **Civil and political:** The Lydian government retains the eastern administrative and transportation centers; critical infrastructure is not needlessly damaged; and coalition actions remain consistent with the defensive mandate.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Overall Concept

The corps conducts an **area defense, organized in depth**, that combines retention of decisive cities and denial of crossings with selective use of space, lateral movement, and mobile counterattack. The **1st Multinational Armored Division** is the initial corps main effort in the Aradesh–Belas sector. The **2nd Multinational Infantry Division** is the supporting effort responsible for Kirov, the Drovna Heights, the Maren approaches, and northern/southern crossings. The **7th Coalition Armored Brigade** is the corps reserve.

The divisions will prevent Vostia from gaining an unopposed route to the Arel while avoiding premature commitment of mobile formations. Once the Vostian main effort or a decisive opportunity becomes apparent, the corps will shift priority and, if required, commit the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade to block or counterattack. The corps will not pursue tactical success beyond the point at which it jeopardizes a decisive city, crossing, or the ability to respond to a second penetration. **Annex C synchronizes the task and purpose of each subordinate formation by phase; the conditions in this paragraph and paragraph 3.f govern transitions.**

#### (2) Phase I — Establish the Defense

This phase begins at H-hour and ends when the principal defensive positions and lateral connections are established and the corps can identify the most dangerous Vostian axis with sufficient confidence, or enemy action forces an earlier transition.

The 1st Division establishes the Aradesh–Belas defense. The 2nd Division establishes mutually supporting positions at Kirov, the Drovna routes, and the southern approaches. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains uncommitted and positioned to reach either division.

#### (3) Phase II — Deny and Fix

This phase begins when the defensive framework is established or Vostian attacks require full defensive action. It ends when a penetration threatens a corps essential task, a decisive counterattack opportunity emerges, or the Vostian offensive culminates.

Divisions retain Aradesh and Kirov, deny the crossings of the Arel River, block the flanks, and use depth and fires to reduce Vostian momentum. Space east of the Arel River may be exchanged for time when doing so preserves combat power and does not surrender an unopposed route to a decisive objective. Priority remains with the 1st Division unless the threat to Kirov or a river crossing becomes imminent at which point priority changes to the defending unit of the threatened city or river crossing.

#### (4) Phase III — Counterattack and Restore

This phase begins when a division or corps counterattack criterion is met. It ends when the enemy penetration is defeated or contained and essential terrain is restored, or continued offensive action would create unacceptable risk.

The 1st and 2nd divisions fix the Vostian force, provide passage for the Corps reserver and flank security, and masses its artillery. The corps committed reserve is the main effort. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains under corps control unless the corps directs a temporary operational-control relationship to the supported division. The counterattacks will restore the defensive framework or destroy a vulnerable operational force, but they will not conduct a pursuit of enemy forces beyond limit of advance (LOA) Steel.

#### (5) Phase IV — Consolidate and Preserve

This phase begins when the immediate threat to Ardesh or the Arel River crossings are controlled. The corps reorganizes, secures cities/routes/crossings, repositions artillery and mobile forces, restores a reserve when possible, and defeats local attempts to renew the Vostian attack. This phase ends at mission completion.

### c. Scheme of Maneuver

The 1st Division conducts an area defense in the central sector to retain Aradesh and Belas, deny the central crossing, and fix the central attack. The 2nd Division conducts an area defense across the northern and southern sectors to retain Kirov, block the restrictive flank routes, and deny the assigned crossings. The seam between the divisions will remain secure and open for lateral support and passage of the corps reserve.

The corps will consider shifting the main effort when Aradesh can be held without priority reinforcement; Kirov is at immediate risk; a Vostian force threatens a usable Arel crossing; a major penetration creates a decisive counterattack opportunity; the initial Vostian main effort culminates; or commitment of a reserve makes that formation the decisive operation.

Before commitment, the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains concentrated under corps control and maintains a primary and alternate movement option toward each division. It updates route status, passage requirements, travel time, and support requirements every 12 hours. It will be committed only by the corps commander. Its primary tactical missions are to block the most dangerous operational avenue or counterattack to defeat or contain a penetration and restore the defense.

When the brigade is committed, the receiving division fixes the Vostian force, establishes movement priority, secures passage and counterattack flanks, provides route and enemy information, coordinates liaison and sustainment, and shifts artillery priority when the brigade becomes the decisive operation. The brigade remains concentrated as a single maneuver force and does not divide between separated axes. The corps specifies the supported division, mission, objective or blocking area, route, release time, command relationship, limit of advance, and termination or return conditions.

### d. Scheme of Fires

The division artillery groups remain in general support of their parent divisions. Initial priority of fires is:

1. support the corps main effort in the Aradesh–Belas sector;
2. disrupt Vostian forces threatening Kirov or an Arel crossing;
3. support a committed division or corps reserve; and
4. preserve artillery survivability and the ability to shift support.

When the corps shifts the main effort or commits the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade, the supported division will shift priority of fires to the decisive operation unless an immediate crossing threat requires otherwise. Fires will disrupt concentrations, blunt penetrations, support counterattack, and deny crossing attempts. Fires are planned and executed under applicable fire-control procedures, rules of engagement, and observed battlefield conditions.

Paragraph 3.e establishes each formation's continuing task and purpose. **Annex C shows how these responsibilities change or continue during each phase.**

#### (1) 1st Multinational Armored Division — Initial Main Effort

- **Task:** Conduct an area defense in the Aradesh–Belas sector; retain Aradesh, and fix the central attack. **Purpose:** Preserve the corps decisive political objective and establish conditions to defeat an armored penetration.
- **Task:** Retain Belas, deny the central river crossing approaches, and secure the northern-central seam and reserve passage route. **Purpose:** Prevent isolation of Aradesh and maintain corps mobility.
- **Be prepared to:** support passage of the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade. **Purpose:** Enable corps decisive action in the central sector.

#### (2) 2nd Multinational Infantry Division — Supporting Effort

- **Task:** Conduct an area defense across the Kirov, Drovna, and Maren–Velin sectors; retain Kirov, block the principal northern and southern turning routes, and deny Vostian use of the Northwest Bridge and designated Arel crossings. **Purpose:** Preserve the transportation network and prevent Vostia from turning either corps flank.
- **Be prepared to:** support passage of the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade. **Purpose:** Enable corps reinforcement or counterattack in the division area.

#### (3) 7th Coalition Armored Brigade — Corps Reserve

- **Task:** Remain concentrated under corps control and prepared to move to either division by a primary or alternate route. **Purpose:** Preserve the corps ability to respond to the Vostian main effort or a second penetration.
- **Task:** Maintain current route, passage, liaison, travel-time, and support plans for both division areas. **Purpose:** Reduce the time between corps release and decisive employment.
- **On Order:** Block the most dangerous operational avenue or counterattack the Vostian penetration. **Purpose:** Defeat or contain the penetration, restore the corps defense, and prevent loss of a decisive city or Arel crossing.
- **Task:** Terminate offensive action when the penetration is defeated, upon reaching LOA Steel, or continued action would exceed supporting fires, expose decisive terrain, or prevent response to a renewed threat. **Purpose:** Convert tactical success into a sustainable corps posture.

#### (3) Commander's Critical Information Requirements

**Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIR)**

1. Where is the Vostian field army main effort, and which armored formation is positioned to exploit?
2. Is a Vostian formation capable of seizing Aradesh, Kirov, or an Arel crossing within the next 24 hours?
3. Is Vostia bypassing a city to attack Belas, Velin, or a northern crossing?
4. Has a Vostian penetration exposed a flank or created an opportunity for decisive counterattack?
5. Is Vostian artillery positioned to mass against a division reserve, crossing, or passage route?

**Friendly Force Information Requirements (FFIR)**

1. Any coalition maneuver formation at or below 70 percent combat power, unable to move, or unable to perform its essential task.
2. Commitment or loss of the 1st Coalition Armored Brigade.
3. Loss, blockage, or interdiction of a route required to move the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade.
4. Loss of coalition/Lydian control of Aradesh, Kirov, Belas, Velin, or a designated Arel crossing.
5. Inability of either division artillery group to support its priority formation.
6. Any action or national restriction that materially limits a coalition formation's assigned mission.

#### (4) Corps-Reserve Release Criteria

The corps commander may release the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade when:

- loss of Aradesh, Kirov, or a usable Arel crossing is imminent;
- an operational penetration threatens to divide the corps or open central Lydia;
- a division reserve is committed and unable to restore the defense;
- a significant Vostian force is fixed and exposed to counterattack with acceptable risk; and
- commitment will not leave a more dangerous axis wholly uncovered.

#### (5) Corps-Reserve Employment Procedures

**Before commitment**

- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains concentrated in assembly area (AA) Hammer with access to both division sectors.
- The brigade reports strength, readiness, primary and alternate routes, estimated travel time, passage requirements, and support shortfalls every 12 hours.
- Reorientation within the reserve area does not constitute commitment. The brigade will not cross a release point or begin the planned combat mission without authenticated corps direction.
- Corps and both divisions maintain current liaison, recognition, passage, fires, sustainment, and route-priority procedures.

**Committing order**

The corps fragmentary order will identify:

1. the criterion or criteria that justify commitment and the risk accepted on other axes;
2. the supported division and the brigade's command relationship after passage;
3. the mission, task and purpose, objective or blocking area, and release time;
4. the primary route, alternate route, passage point, and movement priority;
5. the supporting artillery priority and sustainment relationship;
6. the limit of advance and any terrain that may not be uncovered; and
7. the termination, consolidation, reconstitution, and return-to-corps-control conditions.

**Receiving-division responsibilities**

- Fix or contain the relevant Vostian force and provide current enemy and route information.
- Secure passage, movement routes, penetration shoulders, and at least one counterattack flank.
- Establish traffic priority and prevent conflict with the division reserve or artillery movement.
- Shift artillery priority to the corps reserve when it becomes the decisive operation, unless an immediate crossing threat requires otherwise.
- Provide liaison and coordinate sustainment, medical evacuation, recovery, and terrain handover.

**Execution and termination**

- Preferred employment is a supported counterattack against a significant Vostian force fixed and exposed on a flank. When that opportunity is absent, the brigade blocks the most dangerous avenue or occupies an assigned battle position to retain decisive terrain.
- The brigade will not pursue beyond LOA Steel.
- On termination, the brigade consolidates on defensible terrain and reports whether it can continue the mission, hold assigned terrain, or reconstitute as corps reserve.

**Communications failure**

- Communications failure does not delegate corps-reserve commitment authority.
- Before release, the brigade may reposition locally to avoid destruction, preserve route access, or block an immediate threat to its own position, but it will not initiate the planned corps operation.
- After release, the brigade continues within the last authenticated mission, objective, route, and limit of advance; retains the essential terrain specified in that mission; and reports at the first opportunity.

#### (6) Rules and Constraints

- Operations are limited to internationally recognized Lydian territory.
- Protect civilians, government facilities, bridges, transportation nodes, and energy infrastructure consistent with mission requirements.
- A unit will not abandon a decisive objective or crossing without higher approval unless required to avoid destruction and communications are unavailable; in that event it acts within commander's intent and reports as soon as possible.
- Do not pursue beyond the point at which a counterattack force loses support, exposes a decisive objective, crossed LOA Steel, or cannot respond to a renewed attack.

#### (7) Required Reports

- Contact report after first engagement and upon identification of a new Vostian formation.
- Strength and mission-capability report at 0600 and 1800 local each day and immediately upon crossing an FFIR threshold.
- Objective and crossing-status report whenever control, usability, or access changes.
- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade reports strength, reserve readiness, primary and alternate routes, estimated travel time, passage and liaison status, and support shortfalls every 12 hours until commitment.
- After corps-reserve release, report movement start, passage complete, contact, strength after each engagement, objective or blocking-position status, limit of advance, consolidation, and ability to continue or reconstitute.
- Phase-transition recommendation when a commander assesses conditions are met.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept of Support

Sustainment supports a dispersed defense for 10 days while preserving the ability to shift mobile combat power. Support flows from western Lydian areas through the Arel crossings and along the principal east-west routes. Divisions will avoid placing all support nodes or movement on a single vulnerable crossing.

### b. Priorities of Support

1. Formation designated as corps main effort.
2. Division or corps reserve upon commitment.
3. Units defending Aradesh, Kirov, or a threatened Arel crossing.
4. Artillery groups required to support the decisive operation.
5. Recovery and reconstitution of formations needed for the next phase.

Before release of the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade, corps sustainment planners maintain feasible support options to both division areas. Upon release, the supported division coordinates forward distribution, recovery, casualty evacuation, and route control for the brigade without interrupting support to the terrain-holding force.

### c. Supply and Transportation

Fuel and ammunition priorities support armored movement, defensive combat at decisive objectives, and artillery supporting the main effort. Divisions will maintain primary and alternate supply routes where terrain and route conditions permit and report loss of a route or crossing immediately. Movement of the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade has priority on a corps-designated route once released.

The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade stages fuel, ammunition, maintenance, recovery, medical, and command support for rapid commitment. The supported division confirms that the designated route and passage can support both the brigade and follow-on sustainment before movement begins. During inclement weather, route selection and traffic control account for rain, reduced cross-country mobility, and possible bridge congestion.

### d. Maintenance and Recovery

Priority is restoration of mobile reserves, formations holding decisive terrain, and artillery supporting the main effort. A formation approaching the point of mission failure will not be expended solely to hold nonessential terrain when an orderly displacement can preserve the higher mission.

### e. Medical and Casualty Evacuation

Medical and casualty evacuation are incorporated into parent formations and Lydian rear-area support. Evacuation flows west along available routes. Civilian casualties are coordinated with Lydian authorities where operational conditions permit.

### f. Personnel, Detainees, and Displaced Civilians

Lydian authorities retain primary responsibility for displaced civilians and civil control. Coalition formations report movements that block routes or affect defensive positions. Detainee handling complies with RDC policy and Lydian law and will not divert maneuver formations from essential tasks.

### g. Integrated Support Capabilities

Supply, medical, engineering, air defense, airpower, reconnaissance, electronic warfare, and recovery support are integrated within parent formations and coordinated through the appropriate corps staff. Commanders synchronize these capabilities within assigned authorities and request additional support through command channels.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

#### (1) Command Relationships

- RDC Coalition Land Corps exercises operational control over the 1st and 2nd Divisions and the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade.
- Division artillery groups remain in general support of their parent divisions.
- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains under corps control until corps directs otherwise. If temporarily placed under a division's operational control after commitment, the committing order states the effective time, supported mission, authorities transferred, and conditions for return to corps control. The receiving division may not retask or divide the brigade outside that order without corps approval.

#### (2) Command Posts and Commander Location

The corps main command post remains west of the Arel River it will be initially established in AA Hammer. The corps tactical command element positions to maintain communications with both divisions and to control the decisive operation. It will not displace onto a route required by the corps reserve.

#### (3) Succession of Command

1. RDC Coalition Land Corps commander.
2. Corps deputy commander.
3. Commander, 1st Multinational Armored Division.
4. Commander, 2nd Multinational Infantry Division.

#### (4) Liaison

Each division maintains liaison with the corps and the adjacent division. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade maintains liaison with both divisions before commitment. Multinational formations immediately report interoperability or national-command issues that affect assigned tasks.

### b. Signal

- Primary communications are the RDC tactical command network; alternate methods are national tactical networks routed through liaison teams; contingency methods are prearranged reports, relay, and messenger procedures.
- Commanders will continue within the higher commander's intent during degraded communications. Reserve commitment authority is not delegated by communications failure; the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade may move to preserve itself but will not initiate a corps counterattack without authenticated corps direction.
- Required reports are transmitted by the fastest available method and repeated on restoration of the primary network.
- Friendly recognition, unit identity, and passage procedures follow the coalition standing operating procedure. Adjacent units confirm identity before fires or passage in seam areas.

It supplements Paragraph 3 and does not create a task, authority, objective, or control measure not established in the base order or a subsequent fragmentary order.

## 2. General Instructions

- Phase windows are planning estimates. Conditions and authenticated decisions govern transitions.
- Phase windows may overlap. During transition, each formation continues its existing essential-terrain responsibility until the receiving formation confirms handover.
- Every formation reports when it cannot perform the task shown for the current phase or when a transition condition occurs.
- The corps commander alone releases the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade. This matrix does not constitute release authority.
- Exact routes, release points, passage lanes, boundaries, objectives, blocking areas, and limits of advance are depicted in the operations overlay.

## 4. Principal Decision Points

| Decision point | Decision authority | Required information | Resulting action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **DP 1 — Shift Corps Main Effort** | Corps commander | Threat to Aradesh, Kirov, a crossing, or a flank; enemy main effort; division capability; artillery and route status | Shift priority, liaison, sustainment, and supporting fires to the division facing the greater operational danger or opportunity. |
| **DP 2 — Commit 1st Division Reserve** | 1st Division commander under B-10 criteria | Central penetration, defending-brigade capability, route and passage, flank security, fires | Release the 1st Coalition Armored Brigade to block or counterattack within the division area. |
| **DP 3 — Release Corps Reserve** | Corps commander | Conditions in paragraph 3.f.(4); accepted risk on uncovered axes; receiving-division readiness | Issue the corps-reserve committing fragmentary order described in paragraph 3.f.(5). |
| **DP 4 — Terminate Counterattack** | Corps commander, or supported commander within authenticated limits | Penetration status, decisive terrain, support range, route and flank security, remaining enemy threat | Stop offensive action, occupy defensible terrain, consolidate, and begin recovery or handover. |
| **DP 5 — Reconstitute Reserve** | Corps commander | Brigade strength and mobility, terrain-holding requirement, renewed threat, route access | Return the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade to corps control and reserve status, or retain it on the assigned blocking mission. |

## 5. Annex Reports

- Each division reports current-phase task status, decisive terrain, route and crossing status, reserve or mobile-response status, artillery supportability, and recommended transition twice daily.
- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade reports the pre- and post-commitment information required in paragraph 3.f.(7).
- Any formation immediately reports a conflict between this matrix, the base order, an overlay, or a later authenticated order. The later authenticated order governs, and corps publishes clarification at the first opportunity.
