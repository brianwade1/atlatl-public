## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The division area of interest extends from the northern Drovna routes south through Kirov and the seam with the 1st Multinational Armored Division to the Maren Lowlands and Velin approaches. It includes the northern and southern Arel crossings, routes that could allow Vostian forces to bypass Kirov or Aradesh, and the western routes required for reinforcement by the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade.

Enemy action in the 1st Division sector may draw forces toward the division seam or create a southern threat. Conversely, a Vostian penetration near Kirov or on either flank may force the corps to shift the main effort away from Aradesh.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

- **Kirov transportation hub:** Kirov controls the division's principal east-west and north-south routes. Its retention is essential to lateral movement and connection with the corps central sector.
- **Drovna Heights:** Rough and forested terrain restricts movement and favors light infantry defense, concealment, and economy-of-force operations. Secondary routes can support infiltration or a wide northern turn.
- **Northwest Bridge and northern approaches:** The bridge and adjacent routes provide access across or around the Arel belt. Their loss could turn the Kirov defense and open a route into central Lydia.
- **Maren Lowlands:** Marshes and drainage features restrict maneuver to firm routes. These routes can be blocked by light infantry but may become vulnerable if Vostia masses mechanized forces and supporting artillery.
- **Velin and southern crossings:** Velin and the southern crossing approaches are decisive to preventing a Vostian southern bridgehead.
- **Division frontage:** The division's sectors are separated. The 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade and division artillery cannot be everywhere at once; route status and timely reporting are therefore critical.

### c. Enemy Forces

#### (1) Composition and Disposition

Corps intelligence assesses that the Vostian 7th Maneuver Corps is the principal northern threat. It includes an armored division, a mechanized division, and corps artillery. The mechanized division can attack Kirov and the highway junctions while infantry clears or secures the Drovna routes. Armored forces may penetrate or exploit toward Kirov or the division seam.

Elements of the Vostian 12th Maneuver Corps may threaten the Maren Lowlands and Velin while its main forces operate against Aradesh. Exact boundaries between the Vostian corps and the allocation of their main efforts remain uncertain.

#### (2) Capabilities and Limitations

Vostian forces can attack Kirov directly, use the Drovna Heights to turn the northern flank, advance along firm Maren routes toward Velin, mass artillery against a separated brigade, and exploit a seam between the division and the 1st Division. Armored or mechanized forces can shift more rapidly on the road network than coalition infantry can reposition.

Vostia is constrained by restrictive terrain, limited firm routes, the need to secure routes and occupied areas, and the risk that separated northern and southern efforts will be defeated or blocked individually.

#### (3) Most Likely Course of Action

Vostia attacks toward Kirov with mechanized forces while infantry advances through the Drovna Heights to secure the northern flank and threaten the Northwest Bridge. A supporting force probes or advances through the Maren Lowlands toward Velin to stretch the division and prevent reinforcement of Kirov. Armored forces exploit whichever effort creates the first opening.

#### (4) Most Dangerous Course of Action

Vostia bypasses the Kirov defense simultaneously north and south, seizes or isolates the Northwest Bridge and Velin approaches, cuts the division's lateral routes, and fixes the 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade in Kirov. This would fragment the division and expose more than one Arel crossing before the corps reserve could respond.

### d. Friendly Forces

#### (1) Higher Headquarters Mission and Intent

The RDC Coalition Land Corps conducts an area defense, organized in depth, in eastern Lydia to retain Aradesh and Kirov, deny Vostian use of the Arel crossings, defeat or contain operational penetrations, and preserve a coherent coalition force through the 10-day campaign.

The 2nd Multinational Infantry Division is the initial corps supporting effort. The corps expects the division to retain Kirov, secure the northern transportation network and division seam, block northern and southern turning movements, deny assigned crossings, and request the corps reserve before a separated sector becomes irrecoverable.

#### (2) Adjacent and Supporting Units

- **1st Multinational Armored Division:** Initial corps main effort in the Aradesh–Belas sector. It retains Aradesh and Belas, denies the central crossing, and secures the seam south/east of Kirov. Coordination with its 3rd Coalition Mechanized Brigade is essential.
- **7th Coalition Armored Brigade:** Corps reserve. On corps order, it may block or counterattack in the division area. The division must preserve a route and provide passage, flank security, and coordination.
- **Lydian forces:** Secure rear areas, facilities, portions of the crossing infrastructure, and displaced civilians. They retain these responsibilities and do not relieve division brigades of assigned terrain tasks.

### e. Civil Considerations

Kirov contains transportation-control facilities, government buildings, a divided population, and continuing separatist activity. Civilian movement may congest the same roads needed for lateral reinforcement. Smaller communities in the Drovna and Maren sectors depend on limited routes and crossing infrastructure. Commanders will protect civilians and infrastructure consistent with mission accomplishment and coordinate route control with Lydian authorities.

### f. Attachments and Detachments

None. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains under corps control unless a corps order directs otherwise.

### g. Assumptions

- Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, Velin approaches, and designated crossings begin under coalition/Lydian control and usable unless the operations overlay states otherwise.
- The division receives no additional maneuver formation unless directed by corps.
- The 1st Division retains responsibility for Aradesh and Belas, allowing the 2nd Division to focus on its assigned northern and southern sectors.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local time, the **2nd Multinational Infantry Division conducts an area defense in the Kirov, Drovna, and Maren–Velin sectors to retain Kirov, block the principal northern and southern turning routes, deny Vostian use of the Northwest Bridge and designated Arel crossings, and prevent either corps flank from being turned.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander's Intent

#### Purpose

Prevent Vostia from bypassing the corps central defense and preserve the routes and crossings required to connect eastern Lydia with the remainder of the country.

#### Key Tasks

- Retain Kirov and at least one secure connection to the corps central and western areas.
- Deny Vostian use of the Northwest Bridge and southern crossings near Velin.
- Block firm routes through the Maren Lowlands.
- Prevent a northern turning movement through the Drovna Heights.
- Maintain contact with the 1st Division and protect the division seam.
- Detect and report threats early enough for lateral reinforcement or corps-reserve commitment.
- Shift fires and mechanized combat power without abandoning another decisive responsibility.
- Preserve division combat power and cohesion through 28 May 2030, 0600 local.

#### End State

- **Enemy:** Vostian northern and southern turning efforts are blocked or culminated; no force can exploit through Kirov or a division crossing into central Lydia.
- **Friendly:** The division remains connected across its sectors, Kirov remains defended, and each light brigade retains sufficient combat power to hold its essential terrain.
- **Terrain:** Kirov, the Northwest Bridge/northern approaches, and the southern Arel crossings remain under coalition/Lydian control; Maren routes do not provide an unopposed bypass.
- **Civil and political:** Lydian authority and transportation functions continue in Kirov where possible, critical bridges and infrastructure are protected, and coalition actions remain defensive and within Lydia.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Overall Concept

The division conducts a distributed area defense using light infantry to block restrictive flank routes and mechanized combat power to retain the central transportation hub. The **4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade** is the initial main effort at Kirov. The **5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade** is the northern supporting effort that blocks Drovna routes and denies the Northwest Bridge. The **6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade** is the southern supporting effort that blocks firm Maren routes, retains Velin, and denies designated southern crossings. The **2nd Division Artillery Group** provides general support.

The division will make Vostia fight for canalized routes while preserving Kirov and the ability to shift support. Because the division has no formal reserve formation, early warning and timely corps requests are essential. The 4th Mechanized Brigade may reinforce a threatened flank only on division order and only after the division has addressed continued defense or handover of Kirov. The division will not solve one flank by creating an unopposed route on the other. **Annex C synchronizes the task and purpose of each subordinate formation by phase; the conditions in this paragraph and paragraph 3.f govern transitions.**

#### (2) Phase I — Establish the Defense

**Planning window: 18–19 May 2030.** The 4th Mechanized Brigade occupies positions to retain Kirov and secure its junctions and connections. The 5th Light Infantry Brigade establishes observation and blocks key Drovna routes while denying the Northwest Bridge. The 6th Light Infantry Brigade blocks firm Maren routes, retains Velin, denies designated southern crossings, and secures the seam. Division artillery positions to support the greatest assessed threat and retains options to shift.

This phase ends when essential positions, observation, routes, and fire-support options are established and the division can assess whether the primary threat is Kirov, the north, or the south, or enemy action forces an earlier transition.

#### (3) Phase II — Deny and Fix

**Planning window: 19–24 May 2030.** The 4th Mechanized Brigade retains Kirov; the 5th Light Infantry Brigade blocks the principal Drovna routes and denies the Northwest Bridge; and the 6th Light Infantry Brigade blocks the firm Maren routes, retains Velin, and denies designated southern crossings. The brigades trade nonessential space for time and fix penetrations on identifiable routes. Artillery shifts priority to the sector where a Vostian force can most quickly threaten a city or crossing. The 4th Mechanized Brigade retains Kirov while preparing a limited, specifically tasked lateral response.

This phase ends when a sector is penetrated, a crossing is at imminent risk, the division requires the corps reserve, an opportunity for local counterattack emerges, or the Vostian effort culminates.

#### (4) Phase III — Counterattack and Restore

**Planning window: 21–26 May 2030.** The division assigns the least force necessary a specific tactical task—normally block, fix, or secure—in the threatened sector while retaining Kirov and maintaining the opposite flank's route blocks and crossing denial. The 4th Mechanized Brigade may shift when the division commander establishes how Kirov remains retained. If the corps commits the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade, the threatened brigade fixes the enemy and secures passage and a counterattack flank while artillery shifts priority to the decisive operation.

This phase ends when the threatened route or crossing is restored and the penetration is contained, or when continued counterattack would jeopardize division cohesion.

#### (5) Phase IV — Consolidate and Preserve

**Planning window: 25–27 May 2030.** The division reestablishes sector connections, secures Kirov and the crossings, repositions artillery, restores mechanized mobility, and prepares to defeat renewed local attacks or support corps follow-on operations.

### c. Scheme of Maneuver

The 4th Mechanized Brigade conducts an area defense, organized in depth, centered on Kirov and the principal routes connecting the division to the 1st Division and western Lydia. It retains Kirov and secures its junctions while maintaining one feasible northern or southern response route and updating route status and travel time every 12 hours. It conducts a lateral mission only on authenticated division order that specifies the tactical task and purpose, and only after Kirov is retained by the brigade, handed over, or the division explicitly accepts the risk. If fires or the corps reserve can stabilize the threatened sector without moving the brigade, it remains at Kirov. When ordered to shift, it uses the least force and shortest viable route that preserves its mechanized advantage.

The 5th Light Infantry Brigade conducts an area defense focused on Drovna routes and the Northwest Bridge rather than a continuous line. It maintains depth between observation, route blocks, and the bridge. It displaces from nonessential terrain before isolation while continuing to deny the most dangerous northern route and preserving a connection toward Kirov or the western support area.

The 6th Light Infantry Brigade blocks a network of firm Maren routes, retains Velin, and denies designated southern crossings. It secures the division seam, displaces before a forward block becomes isolated, and continues to block the most dangerous southern route and deny the assigned crossing. Both light brigades retain their crossing responsibilities unless the division explicitly changes them and must identify routes suitable for mechanized or armored movement.

The division requests the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade when imminent loss of Kirov or a crossing exceeds division capability, when a penetration threatens to divide the corps, or when a significant Vostian force is fixed and exposed to decisive counterattack. The division does not assume the corps reserve is available until corps directs commitment. When committed in the division area, the threatened brigade fixes the enemy; the division establishes movement priority, secures passage and at least one counterattack flank, provides route and enemy information, coordinates sustainment and terrain handover, and shifts artillery priority when the corps reserve becomes the decisive operation.

### d. Scheme of Fires

The 2nd Division Artillery Group remains in general support. Because the sectors are separated, priority of fires is conditional rather than permanently tied to one brigade.

Initial priority:

1. 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade at Kirov when a direct attack can seize the transportation hub.
2. 5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade when a northern force threatens the Northwest Bridge or can turn Kirov.
3. 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade when a mechanized force threatens Velin or a southern crossing.
4. 7th Coalition Armored Brigade if corps commits it as the decisive operation in the division area.

Priority shifts to the sector in which the enemy can reach a decisive objective soonest, or to the committed counterattack force. Fires disrupt canalized movement, blunt attacks on Kirov or crossings, and enable reinforcement and counterattack. The artillery formation cannot provide simultaneous full support to widely separated sectors unless the current position, range, and supportability permit it.

#### (3) Commander's Critical Information Requirements

**PIR**

1. Is the Vostian northern main effort directed at Kirov, the Drovna routes, or the Northwest Bridge?
2. Is a Vostian mechanized or armored formation committed to the Maren–Velin corridor?
3. Can a Vostian force reach Kirov or a division crossing within 24 hours?
4. Is Vostia attempting simultaneous northern and southern bypasses?
5. Has a Vostian force become fixed and exposed to corps counterattack?

**FFIR**

1. Any brigade at or below 70 percent combat power, isolated, or unable to perform its essential task.
2. Loss or imminent loss of Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, Velin approaches, or a designated crossing.
3. Loss or blockage of the route connecting a light brigade to Kirov or the western support area.
4. 4th Mechanized Brigade unable to defend Kirov and conduct an ordered lateral response.
5. Inability of division artillery to support the priority sector.
6. Requirement for the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade or a change in division boundaries.

#### (4) Reinforcement and Corps-Reserve Request Criteria

The division will request corps-reserve commitment when:

- Kirov or a usable crossing is at imminent risk beyond the defending brigade's capability;
- an operational penetration threatens to fragment the division or divide the corps;
- shifting the 4th Mechanized Brigade would create an unacceptable risk at Kirov; or
- a significant Vostian force is fixed and vulnerable to decisive counterattack.

The 4th Mechanized Brigade may shift from Kirov only when the division commander determines the continued defense or handover of Kirov and confirms a viable route to the threatened sector.

#### (5) Brigade Movement, Displacement, and Passage Rules

**4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade**

- The brigade is not a formal reserve. It remains responsible for Kirov while preparing a conditional lateral response.
- It reports route status, travel time, strength, Kirov retention requirements, and ability to conduct a specified tactical task on either flank twice daily.
- It moves laterally only on authenticated division order after the division specifies Kirov's continued retention or accepted risk, the supported brigade, route, doctrinal task and purpose, termination condition, and return or follow-on task.
- Use the least force and shortest viable route required to stabilize the threatened sector. Avoid restrictive terrain that eliminates mechanized advantage.
- Terminate the lateral mission when the route or crossing is restored, the penetration is contained, or further movement creates unacceptable risk at Kirov or another sector.

**5th and 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigades**

- Conduct area defense of networks of routes and crossing approaches rather than continuous forward lines.
- Displace from nonessential positions before isolation while continuing to deny the most dangerous route or crossing and reporting the next denial position.
- Do not abandon the Northwest Bridge, Velin, or a designated crossing responsibility without authenticated division direction or an immediate survival action under degraded communications.
- Preserve a route for sustainment, evacuation, mechanized reinforcement, or corps-reserve passage and report changes immediately.
- When reinforced, fix the enemy, provide current route and threat information, secure passage and a designated flank, and retain the essential crossing task unless the division changes it.

**Corps-reserve reception**

- When the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade is committed, comply with the receiving-division responsibilities in B-00 paragraph 3.f.(5).
- Establish route priority, prevent conflict with artillery or 4th Brigade movement, and confirm passage and flank security before the corps reserve crosses the release point.
- The division may not divide or retask the corps reserve outside the corps committing order.

#### (6) Risk Controls and Constraints

- Do not abandon Kirov to solve a flank threat without division and corps coordination.
- Do not withdraw a light brigade from a crossing without reporting the change and coordinating the next denial position.
- Avoid moving mechanized reinforcements onto terrain or routes that eliminate their mobility advantage.
- Do not assume artillery can support both flanks simultaneously.
- Protect civilians, bridges, government facilities, and transportation infrastructure consistent with the mission.
- A brigade may displace to avoid destruction when communications fail, but it will continue to deny its essential route or crossing where possible and report at the first opportunity.

#### (7) Required Reports

- Contact and identified-enemy report after each new engagement.
- Strength and mission-capability report at 0600 and 1800 local each day and upon crossing an FFIR threshold.
- Kirov, bridge, route, and crossing status whenever control, usability, or access changes.
- 4th Mechanized Brigade Kirov status, northern and southern route condition, travel times, strength, and lateral-response capability every 12 hours.
- Light-brigade observation, denial-position, support/evacuation-route, crossing, isolation-risk, reinforcement-passage, and next-position status every 12 hours and whenever changed.
- Corps-reserve passage, movement, contact, objective, limit-of-advance, and consolidation status when committed in the division area.
- Priority-of-fires shift and phase-transition recommendations.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept of Support

Division support operates from west of the Arel and moves through available northern and southern routes. The division will distribute risk so loss of one route or crossing does not halt support to all sectors. Sustainment priorities follow the threat to decisive terrain and must preserve the 4th Mechanized Brigade's mobility.

### b. Priorities of Support

1. Brigade defending the objective under the most immediate threat.
2. 4th Mechanized Brigade when conducting an ordered lateral response.
3. 7th Coalition Armored Brigade upon commitment in the division area.
4. 2nd Division Artillery Group supporting the decisive operation.
5. Recovery and reconstitution of brigades required to continue crossing defense.

### c. Supply and Transportation

Fuel priority supports the 4th Mechanized Brigade and any committed corps reserve. Ammunition priority supports the brigade holding the most threatened city, route, or crossing and the artillery group supporting it. Each brigade identifies primary and alternate support routes where terrain and route conditions permit and reports route loss immediately.

Before 21 May 2030, 0600 local, brigades confirm reinforcement and evacuation routes and pre-position critical supplies without blocking Kirov junctions or crossing approaches. During 21–23 May 2030, movement plans account for rain, reduced Drovna route usability, saturated Maren terrain, bridge congestion, and possible conflict among the 4th Brigade, corps reserve, artillery, and sustainment traffic.

### d. Maintenance and Recovery

Priority is the formation defending the most threatened decisive objective, followed by the 4th Mechanized Brigade and artillery. Recovery will preserve formations from avoidable elimination when displacement remains consistent with the assigned route or crossing mission.

### e. Medical and Casualty Evacuation

Casualty evacuation flows west along available sector routes. When a route is cut, the affected brigade coordinates an alternate route through Kirov, a neighboring sector, or the corps support area. Formations use organic medical assets and coordinate additional support through division medical channels.

### f. Personnel, Detainees, and Displaced Civilians

Lydian authorities retain primary responsibility for displaced civilians and civil control. Brigade commanders report population movement that blocks a route or crossing. Detainee handling follows RDC policy and Lydian law and will not create a new maneuver task for a brigade formation.

### g. Integrated Support Capabilities

Supply, medical, engineer, reconnaissance, air defense, aviation, airpower, electronic warfare, and recovery support are integrated within parent formations and coordinated through the division staff. Commanders synchronize these capabilities within assigned authorities and request additional support through corps channels.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

#### (1) Command Relationships

- The 2nd Multinational Infantry Division has operational control of the 4th, 5th, and 6th Coalition Brigades.
- The 2nd Division Artillery Group is in general support.
- The division has no formal reserve formation. The 4th Mechanized Brigade remains the Kirov defender and mobile response force as directed.
- The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade remains under corps control unless corps directs a temporary transfer after commitment. If transferred, the division may not divide or retask it outside the corps committing order and returns it under the stated termination conditions.

#### (2) Command Posts and Commander Location

The division main command post remains west of Kirov along a route that preserves communications with both flanks and access to the corps support area. The division tactical command element positions near the sector assessed as most dangerous without displacing onto a route required for reinforcement.

#### (3) Succession of Command

1. Commander, 2nd Multinational Infantry Division.
2. Division deputy commander.
3. Commander, 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade.
4. Commander, 5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade.
5. Commander, 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade.

#### (4) Liaison

The division maintains liaison with corps and the 1st Division. The 4th Brigade maintains coordination with both light brigades. If the corps reserve is oriented toward the division, liaison will be exchanged before movement. Brigade liaison reports route travel times, crossing status, and interoperability constraints.

### b. Signal

- Primary communications are the division coalition network; alternate communications use national tactical networks through liaison teams; contingency communications use relay, scheduled reports, and messenger procedures.
- Subordinate commanders continue within intent during degraded communications. A brigade protects its assigned city, route, or crossing and avoids irreversible movement that exposes another sector without authenticated direction.
- Crossing, route, FFIR, and reinforcement reports have precedence.

# Annex C (Operations): Division Synchronization Matrix

## 1. Purpose

This annex synchronizes the tasks and purposes of the 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade, 5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade, 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade, and 2nd Division Artillery Group across the division phases. It supplements Paragraph 3 and does not create an authority, objective, route, crossing, or control measure not established in the base order or a later authenticated order.

## 2. General Instructions

- Phase windows are estimates; conditions and authenticated decisions govern transitions.
- Phase windows may overlap. A brigade retains its assigned city, route, seam, or crossing until the receiving formation confirms handover.
- The division has no formal reserve formation. A task to prepare for lateral response does not release the 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade from defending Kirov.
- Light brigades may displace from nonessential terrain before isolation but continue their essential route and crossing-denial purposes.
- Exact boundaries, blocking and denial positions, routes, release points,
  passage lanes, crossings, artillery areas, and limits of advance will be
  published in the operations overlay or a subsequent fragmentary order.
