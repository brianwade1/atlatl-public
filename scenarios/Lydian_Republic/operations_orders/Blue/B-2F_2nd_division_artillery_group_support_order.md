- **Date-time group:** 18 May 2030, 0600 local
- **Time zone:** Local Lydian time
- **Effective:** On receipt
- **Operation period:** 18 May 2030, 0600 – 28 May 2030, 0600 local
- **Revision:** Doctrinal mission-task alignment edition
- **Parent order:** 2nd Multinational Infantry Division OPORD B-20, OPERATION GUARDED PASSAGES
- **Support relationship:** General support to 2nd Multinational Infantry Division
- **Supported higher tasks:** B-C2, B-C3, B-C4, B-C5, B-C6

### Task Organization

The group operates as an integrated artillery formation. Organic command, target acquisition, survey, air defense, sustainment, maintenance, medical, recovery, and communications functions remain internal. No attachments or detachments are in effect at H-hour.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The area of interest extends from Vostian assembly and artillery areas east of Kirov through the Drovna routes and Northwest Bridge in the north, the Kirov approaches and division seam in the center, and the Maren–Velin routes and southern crossings. It includes enemy forces capable of threatening any decisive objective within 24 hours and routes required for division or corps reinforcement.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

The division frontage is separated by restrictive terrain and distance. Drovna heights restrict observation and displacement; Kirov provides central road access but may be targeted; Maren routes channel artillery movement and resupply onto firm surfaces. The artillery group cannot assume simultaneous full support to both flanks. Firing positions must support the priority sector while retaining displacement routes and avoiding congestion with maneuver or corps-reserve movement.

### c. Enemy Forces

Vostian corps and division artillery can mass against separated brigades, interdict Kirov’s junctions, and disrupt movement of the 4th Brigade or corps reserve. High-value threats include artillery supporting the enemy main effort, mechanized/armored concentrations approaching Kirov or a crossing, forces securing penetration shoulders, and units blocking friendly reinforcement routes.

### d. Friendly Forces

The 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade retains Kirov and secures its junctions while remaining the principal conditional lateral-response force. The 5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade blocks principal Drovna routes and denies the Northwest Bridge. The 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade blocks firm Maren routes, retains Velin, and denies designated southern crossings. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade may become the decisive corps operation. The artillery group remains in general support under centralized division control.

### e. Civil Considerations

Kirov, Velin, bridge approaches, and smaller communities contain civilians and critical transport infrastructure. Fires require positive identification and must avoid unnecessary damage to crossings, government, medical, and transport facilities needed for defense and civil support.

### f. Assumptions

Observer, air, reconnaissance, target-acquisition, and ammunition support are integrated within the group and supporting headquarters. Firing areas, ranges, and target restrictions are established by the operations overlay, rules of engagement, and applicable fire-control procedures.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local, the **2nd Division Artillery Group provides general support to the 2nd Multinational Infantry Division, disrupts the most dangerous Vostian attack, isolates a decisive penetration, suppresses threats to retained terrain, crossings, passage, and committed maneuver forces, and shifts priority to the division or corps decisive operation in order to enable retention of Kirov and Velin, block turning routes, and deny Vostian use of assigned Arel crossings.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander’s Intent

**Purpose:** Apply limited artillery effects at the most operationally dangerous point of the division’s distributed defense.

**Key tasks:** Maintain coverage options for Kirov, Northwest Bridge, and Velin/southern crossings; identify the priority sector every 12 hours; mass rather than dilute fires; support lateral and corps-reserve movement; preserve ammunition, communications, and survivability; displace before losing range or access.

**End state:** The most dangerous Vostian effort is disrupted or fixed; maneuver brigades receive timely support; reinforcement routes remain usable; the group survives with sufficient capability through 28 May 2030, 0600 local.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### Phase I — Establish the Defense (18–19 May 2030)

Provide general support with initial priority to the sector where the assessed threat can reach decisive terrain soonest; at H-hour, the 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade at Kirov is the division main effort. Occupy overlay-designated positions supporting that threat while preserving alternate positions and routes toward north, center, and south. Confirm communications, target priorities, support ranges, displacement routes, and travel times with each brigade. This phase ends when coverage, routes, communications, and response estimates are established or enemy action requires immediate transition.

#### Phase II — Deny and Fix (19–24 May 2030)

Shift priority to the brigade responsible for the objective an enemy force can reach soonest. Disrupt concentrations, separate enemy echelons on restrictive routes, and suppress threats to Kirov, Velin, and assigned crossing approaches while retaining route access, range, and survivability. Report immediately when support to one sector prevents timely support to another. Maintain centralized control; do not establish independent support elements for separated sectors without division approval. This phase ends on penetration, imminent loss of Kirov or a crossing, a corps-reserve request, a counterattack opportunity, Vostian culmination, or transition to consolidation.

#### Phase III — Counterattack and Restore (21–26 May 2030)

Support an authenticated 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade tactical mission or shift priority to the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade when corps commits it and designates it the decisive operation. Suppress threats to passage and counterattack flanks, isolate the penetration, continue essential crossing-denial fires, and displace to maintain range and survivability. Support termination and consolidation without assuming full simultaneous support to separated sectors. This phase ends when the threatened route or crossing is restored and the penetration contained, or further action would fragment the division or exceed support capability.

#### Phase IV — Consolidate and Preserve (25–27 May 2030)

Reposition in general support to suppress renewed threats to retained terrain, decisive routes, and crossing approaches. Replenish, recover, preserve survivability, and prepare to disrupt renewed attacks through 28 May 2030, 0600 local. This phase ends when the division end state is achieved at mission completion or a new order is received.

### c. Scheme of Fires

Conditional priority hierarchy:

1. The brigade defending the objective the enemy can reach soonest; at H-hour, the 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade at Kirov.
2. 5th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade when a northern force threatens the Northwest Bridge or can turn Kirov.
3. 6th Coalition Light Infantry Brigade when a mechanized force threatens Velin or a southern crossing.
4. 4th Coalition Mechanized Brigade when conducting an authenticated lateral response.
5. 7th Coalition Armored Brigade when corps commits it and designates it the decisive operation in the division area.

When threats are simultaneous, support the enemy force that can reach decisive terrain soonest; the division commander resolves ties and directs priority shifts. The group recommends a shift whenever threat, range, route, or survivability changes. Target priorities are enemy artillery supporting the main effort; armored/mechanized concentrations; leading forces on canalized routes; penetration shoulders; and units threatening crossing or passage areas. The group remains centrally controlled and cannot promise full simultaneous support to widely separated sectors. Effects are assessed from observed results under approved fire-control procedures.

### d. Scheme of Movement and Survivability

Maintain range to the current priority sector and one feasible displacement route. Begin wet-weather movement early enough to avoid interrupting support when practicable. Coordinate all displacement with division movement control so the artillery formation does not block 4th Brigade or corps-reserve passage. Avoid unnecessary forward displacement into restrictive terrain.

### e. Tasks to Organic Elements

- **Task:** Maintain target acquisition on enemy artillery, decisive-route concentrations, and crossing threats. **Purpose:** Enable timely massing of fires against the threat closest to decisive terrain.
- **Task:** Maintain communications with the division and all maneuver brigades. **Purpose:** Execute rapid and authenticated priority shifts across separated sectors.
- **Task:** Prepare primary and alternate firing and displacement areas consistent with the operations overlay. **Purpose:** Preserve range, route access, and survivability.
- **Task:** Preserve ammunition for decisive defense and reinforcement periods. **Purpose:** Sustain fires for the threatened brigade or committed corps reserve.
- **Task:** Maintain local security, maintenance, recovery, medical support, and route awareness internally. **Purpose:** Preserve continuous artillery support across the division area.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Fire-Support Triggers

- Support Kirov when a direct attack can seize the hub.
- Shift north when an identified force can reach the Northwest Bridge or turn Kirov.
- Shift south when a mechanized force can reach Velin or a crossing.
- Support 4th Brigade on authenticated lateral-movement order.
- Support 7th Armored Brigade only upon corps/division direction designating it the decisive operation.
- If a priority shift cannot be executed without losing range, route access, or survivability, report the limitation and earliest support time immediately.

#### (2) Constraints

- Do not assume simultaneous full support to widely separated sectors.
- Confirm friendly locations before fires near seams, passages, crossings, or populated areas.
- Coalition fires remain within authorized defensive operations in Lydia.
- Do not claim effects that have not been observed or confirmed by the supported headquarters.

#### (3) CCIR

**PIR:** Enemy main effort and supporting artillery; force capable of reaching a decisive objective within 24 hours; simultaneous northern/southern threats; penetration exposed to counterattack; enemy action forcing displacement.

**FFIR:** Group at or below 70 percent combat power; unable to range/support the priority sector; communications lost with the supported brigade; ammunition or mobility prevents priority missions; firing position compromised; displacement route blocked; passage conflict delaying support more than 12 hours.

#### (4) Reports

Report every 12 hours: current priority formation; coverage and readiness; range and route status; displacement location, start, and completion; survivability; route travel time; strength and mission capability; ammunition concern; and ability to support the current and next anticipated decisive action. Report acquired high-value threats, recommended and directed priority shifts, any inability to support the current or next decisive action, passage conflicts, and all FFIR immediately. These reports feed B-20 Annex C decision points and phase transitions.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept

Sustainment operates from west of the Arel and follows the priority sector without congesting the limited routes. Distribution preserves the ability to shift between separated areas.

### b. Priorities

Ammunition for the most threatened objective; fuel and maintenance for displacement; communications and target acquisition; recovery; casualty evacuation; replenishment for renewed defense.

### c. Transportation, Maintenance, and Medical

Coordinate artillery traffic with the 4th Brigade and corps reserve. During 21–23 May 2030, allow additional time for ammunition movement and displacement. Repair priority goes to systems that preserve effective range, communications, and fires capability. Avoid preventable loss below the point of mission failure. Casualties evacuate west along the current support route.

### d. Medical and Casualty Evacuation

Organic medical personnel provide point-of-injury care and coordinate
evacuation west along the current support route through division medical
channels.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains in general support under division control. The command post occupies an overlay-designated protected position that enables centralized control and lateral displacement. Succession is group commander, deputy commander, then senior organic fires commander. Liaison remains with division and all maneuver brigades; liaison with 7th Armored Brigade begins when it is oriented into the division area.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use the division fires and coalition networks; alternate communications use national networks through liaison; contingency communications use relay, prearranged schedules, and messenger. Threat-to-objective reports, priority shifts, passage movement, and FFIR have precedence. Under degraded communications, support the last confirmed priority sector and do not fire near uncertain friendly positions.
