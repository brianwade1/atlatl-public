# 1st Division Artillery Group

> **FOR WARGAMING AND EDUCATIONAL USE ONLY — ENTIRELY FICTIONAL**

- **Issuing headquarters:** 1st Division Artillery Group
- **Date-time group:** 18 May 2030, 0600 local
- **Time zone:** Local Lydian time
- **Effective:** On receipt
- **Operation period:** 18 May 2030, 0600 – 28 May 2030, 0600 local
- **Revision:** Doctrinal mission-task alignment edition
- **Parent order:** 1st Multinational Armored Division OPORD B-10, OPERATION CENTRAL SHIELD
- **Support relationship:** General support to 1st Multinational Armored Division
- **Supported higher tasks:** B-C1, B-C3, B-C5, B-C6

### Task Organization

The group operates as an integrated artillery formation. Organic command, target acquisition, survey, air defense, sustainment, maintenance, medical, and communications functions remain internal. No attachments or detachments are in effect at H-hour.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The area of interest includes Vostian assembly and artillery areas east of Aradesh, the Aradesh Plain, northern and southern bypass routes, Belas and the central Arel approaches, reserve movement corridors, and targets affecting the seam with the 2nd Division.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

Open ground around Aradesh offers observation but little concealment. Urban terrain restricts observation and increases the need for positive target identification. The Aradesh–Belas route supports displacement and resupply but may also carry reserves and civilians. Firing positions must support Aradesh, Belas, and anticipated counterattack areas while retaining survivability and displacement options.

### c. Enemy Forces

Vostian corps and divisional artillery can suppress defenders, interdict the Aradesh–Belas route, and disrupt reserve movement. Maneuver forces are expected to fix Aradesh and exploit toward Belas. High-value threats include artillery supporting the main attack, armored concentrations, forces on penetration shoulders, and units threatening the crossing or friendly counterattack.

### d. Friendly Forces

The division conducts an area defense, organized in depth, in the Aradesh–Belas sector. The 2nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade is the initial main effort to retain Aradesh and fix the central attack; the 3rd Brigade retains Belas, denies the central crossing, and secures the seam and passage routes; the 1st Armored Brigade is division reserve and becomes the main effort upon commitment to block or counterattack. The 7th Coalition Armored Brigade may become the corps decisive operation in this area. The artillery group remains in general support and retains centralized control to mass limited fires.

### e. Civil Considerations

Aradesh and Belas contain civilians and critical infrastructure. Fires require positive identification and proportional effects appropriate to the fictional operation. Avoid unnecessary damage to crossing, government, medical, and transport infrastructure needed for defense and civil support.

### f. Assumptions

Observer, air, reconnaissance, target-acquisition, and ammunition support are integrated within the group and supporting headquarters. Firing areas and ranges are established by the operations overlay and applicable fire-control procedures.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local, the **1st Division Artillery Group provides general support to the 1st Multinational Armored Division, disrupts Vostian concentrations, isolates the decisive penetration, suppresses threats to passage and counterattack flanks, and shifts priority to the committed division or corps counterattack in order to enable retention of Aradesh and Belas, deny Vostian use of the central Arel crossing, and preserve the division's freedom of action.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander’s Intent

**Purpose:** Place responsive, survivable fires at the division’s decisive point throughout the ten-day operation.

**Key tasks:** Maintain coverage of Aradesh, Belas, and counterattack routes; mass rather than disperse effects; preserve ammunition and survivability; shift priority quickly; avoid fratricide and unnecessary civil damage; displace before losing support range or survivability.

**End state:** Maneuver brigades receive timely support at decisive moments; Vostian concentrations and penetrations are disrupted; the crossing remains denied; the group survives with sufficient capability for final consolidation.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### Phase I — Establish the Defense (18–19 May 2030)

Provide general support with initial priority to the 2nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade at Aradesh. Occupy overlay-designated firing positions that cover Aradesh while retaining the ability to shift to Belas or support a released reserve. Confirm communications, target priorities, primary and alternate displacement routes, support ranges, and passage coordination with all maneuver brigades. This phase ends when coverage, routes, communications, and support options are established or enemy action requires immediate transition.

#### Phase II — Deny and Fix (19–24 May 2030)

Continue priority to the 2nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade, disrupt enemy concentrations, separate assault and exploitation echelons, and suppress threats to the retained Aradesh–Belas connection. Shift priority to the 3rd Coalition Mechanized Brigade if Belas or the central crossing is directly threatened. Preserve survivability, route access, ammunition, and range needed for the next decisive action. This phase ends when division reserve-release criteria occur, corps-reserve action begins, Vostian forces culminate, or the division can consolidate without counterattack.

#### Phase III — Counterattack and Restore (21–26 May 2030)

Upon authenticated division release of the 1st Coalition Armored Brigade, shift priority to its operation unless immediate Belas crossing denial takes precedence. If corps commits the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade and designates it the decisive operation in the division area, shift priority when directed. Support passage, isolate the penetration, suppress threats on the objective and flanks, maintain range through displacement, and cover termination and consolidation. This phase ends when the penetration is defeated or contained and the defensive framework is restored, or continued action would expose Aradesh or Belas or exceed support capability.

#### Phase IV — Consolidate and Preserve (25–27 May 2030)

Reposition in general support to suppress renewed threats to retained terrain, decisive routes, and crossing approaches. Replenish, recover, preserve survivability, and prepare to disrupt renewed attacks through 28 May 2030, 0600 local. This phase ends when the division end state is achieved at mission completion or a new order is received.

### c. Scheme of Fires

Priority hierarchy:

1. 2nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade retaining Aradesh and fixing the central attack.
2. 3rd Coalition Mechanized Brigade if Belas or the central crossing is directly threatened.
3. 1st Coalition Armored Brigade after authenticated division release, unless immediate Belas crossing denial takes precedence.
4. 7th Coalition Armored Brigade when corps commits it and designates it the decisive operation in the division area, unless immediate Belas crossing denial takes precedence.

The division commander directs priority shifts; the group recommends a shift whenever threat, range, route, or survivability changes. Target priorities are enemy artillery directly supporting the main attack; armored/mechanized concentrations; forces threatening Aradesh–Belas connectivity; penetration shoulders and reserves; and forces threatening the central crossing. The group retains central control to mass effects and does not promise simultaneous priority support to separated actions. Fire missions, ranges, and effects follow approved fire-control procedures and observed results.

### d. Scheme of Movement and Survivability

Remain within support range of the current main effort and maintain at least one displacement route west or laterally. Displace when enemy threat, range, route closure, or phase transition requires it. Coordinate movement so artillery does not block a released reserve. Wet-weather displacement begins early enough to prevent 12 hours of lost support when practicable.

### e. Tasks to Organic Elements

- **Task:** Maintain target acquisition focused on enemy artillery, exploitation forces, and crossing threats. **Purpose:** Enable timely massing of fires against the division's most dangerous threat.
- **Task:** Maintain communications with the division and supported brigades. **Purpose:** Execute rapid and authenticated priority shifts.
- **Task:** Prepare primary and alternate firing and displacement areas consistent with the operations overlay. **Purpose:** Preserve range, survivability, and support to the next decisive action.
- **Task:** Preserve ammunition for decisive defense and counterattack periods. **Purpose:** Sustain fires when the division commits its reserve or defends essential terrain.
- **Task:** Maintain local security, air-defense awareness, maintenance, recovery, and casualty care internally. **Purpose:** Preserve continuous artillery support to the division.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Fire-Support Triggers

- Mass against an identified enemy formation preparing or conducting a decisive assault on Aradesh.
- Shift to Belas when a force can directly attack the crossing or sever the route.
- Shift to 1st Armored Brigade upon authenticated division release and passage confirmation.
- Shift to 7th Armored Brigade only on corps/division direction designating it the decisive operation.
- If a priority shift cannot be executed without losing range, route access, or survivability, report the limitation and earliest support time immediately.

#### (2) Constraints

- Coalition forces and fires remain within authorized operations in Lydian territory; no general attack into Vostia.
- Confirm friendly locations before fires near seams, passage lanes, or urban positions.
- Do not claim effects that have not been observed or confirmed by the supported headquarters.
- Protect crossing and essential civil infrastructure unless the immediate military necessity is coordinated otherwise.

#### (3) CCIR

**PIR:** Enemy artillery supporting the main effort; armored concentration and direction; force threatening Belas/crossing; penetration shoulders; enemy action forcing displacement.

**FFIR:** Group at or below 70 percent combat power; unable to range or support Aradesh, Belas, or the committed counterattack; communications lost with main effort; ammunition or mobility state prevents priority missions; firing position compromised; displacement route blocked.

#### (4) Reports

Report every 12 hours: current priority formation; coverage and readiness; range and route status; displacement location, start, and completion; survivability; strength and mission capability; ammunition concern; and ability to support the current and next anticipated decisive action. Report significant enemy target acquisition, directed or recommended priority shifts, fire-mission completion per standard reporting procedures, any inability to support the current or next decisive action, and all FFIR immediately. These reports feed B-10 Annex C decision points and phase transitions.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept

Sustainment keeps the group responsive and mobile while avoiding congestion on the Aradesh–Belas route. Ammunition and fuel distribution follow division priorities and anticipated decisive periods.

### b. Priorities

Ammunition for crossing denial and decisive maneuver support; fuel and maintenance for displacement; communications and target acquisition; recovery; medical support; replenishment for renewed defense.

### c. Transportation and Maintenance

Coordinate artillery movement with reserve and sustainment traffic. During 21–23 May 2030, allow additional time for movement and resupply. Repair priority goes to systems necessary to retain range, communications, and overall combat capability. Avoid preventable loss below the point of mission failure.

### d. Medical and Casualty Evacuation

Casualties evacuate west through Belas or a division-approved alternate
route. Organic medical personnel provide point-of-injury care and
coordinate evacuation through division medical channels.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains in general support under division control. The artillery command post occupies an overlay-designated position that supports centralized fire control and displacement. Succession is group commander, deputy commander, then senior organic fires commander. Liaison remains with division and each maneuver brigade; liaison with 7th Armored Brigade begins if it is oriented into the division area.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use the division fires and coalition networks; alternate communications use national networks through liaison; contingency communications use relay, prearranged schedules, and messenger. Fire-priority shifts, crossing threats, reserve release, friendly passage, and FFIR have precedence. Under degraded communications, support the last confirmed main effort and do not fire near uncertain friendly positions.
