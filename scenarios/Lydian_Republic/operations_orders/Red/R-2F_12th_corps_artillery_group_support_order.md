### Task Organization

The group is represented by one aggregate artillery formation in Atlatl. Command, target acquisition, communications, survey, sustainment, maintenance, recovery, medical, air-defense awareness, and local security remain internal. No subordinate batteries, calibers, or ammunition quantities are separately represented.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Operations

The group supports operations across the Aradesh Plain, Maren Lowlands, Aradesh, Belas, Velin, the Maren Bridge approaches, and the 12th Corps portion of PL Jab. Open ground favors observation but exposes firing positions. Marsh and restricted roads in the Maren sector constrain displacement. As the corps advances, the Aradesh–Belas and Velin directions separate; the aggregate artillery formation cannot assume full simultaneous coverage of every objective, guard sector, and route.

Exact firing positions, ranges, target-reference points, support areas, routes, and other map-dependent controls not shown on the approved overlay remain `TBD—SCENARIO DESIGN`.

### b. Weather and Light

The R-20 forecast applies. Dry conditions through Turn 6 favor observation and displacement. Rain during Turns 7–12 increases dependence on firm roads, slows movement and resupply, and raises the risk of congestion between artillery, maneuver, reserve, and sustainment traffic. Fog can conceal displacement but delay target acquisition. Weather effects not native to Atlatl are narrative or consistently adjudicated.

### c. Enemy Forces

Lydian and RDC Coalition artillery and maneuver formations can defend Aradesh, Belas, Velin, and the Maren Bridge; reinforce along central or southern routes; counterattack north from the Maren Lowlands; and strike artillery positions or displacement routes. The most dangerous enemy action is a coordinated counterattack from the Maren Lowlands against the Aradesh Plain while the group is oriented on the Velin attack or displacing between support areas.

### d. Friendly Forces

- **12th Maneuver Corps:** Field army main effort throughout. Seizes Aradesh and Belas with the 18th Armored Division, shifts corps main effort to the 22nd Mechanized Division to seize Velin in Phase III, then returns main effort to the 18th Division for the Phase IV guard.
- **18th Armored Division:** Corps main effort in Phases I–II and IV. The 14th Armored Brigade attacks Aradesh and later guards the southern Aradesh Plain; the 15th Armored Brigade clears south of Aradesh, seizes and retains Belas; the 23rd Mechanized Brigade retains Aradesh.
- **22nd Mechanized Division:** Corps main effort in Phase III. The 32nd Infantry Brigade develops and fixes the southern enemy, seizes and retains Velin; the 24th Mechanized Brigade is corps reserve in Phases I–III and conducts the southern PL Jab guard after release in Phase IV.

### e. Civil Considerations

Preserve essential infrastructure and routes in Aradesh, Belas, Velin, and at the Maren Bridge when consistent with the mission. Confirm target and friendly locations before fires near cities, bridges, seams, retains, handovers, or uncertain front lines.

### f. Assumptions

- The group remains one aggregate counter and cannot provide full simultaneous support to separated Aradesh, Belas, Velin, and guard actions.
- Range, target eligibility, movement, and combat effects follow Atlatl mechanics and agreed scenario adjudication.
- The corps commander authenticates changes in priority of fires.
- A phase transition does not by itself release the 24th Mechanized Brigade or change fires priority.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local, the **12th Corps Artillery Group provides general support to the 12th Maneuver Corps; gives priority of fires to the 18th Armored Division during Phases I–II, shifts priority to the 22nd Mechanized Division during Phase III, and returns priority to the 18th Armored Division during Phase IV; shifts temporarily on corps order to the most dangerous counterattack or committed reserve; and displaces to maintain support through PL Jab in order to enable seizure and retention of Aradesh, Belas, and Velin and defeat coalition attempts to reverse the corps advance.**

- Maintain sufficient fires to support the 14th Brigade guard against counterattacks moving north from the Maren Lowlands.
- Return priority to the 18th Division guard in Phase IV and support the 22nd Division guard as next priority.
- Displace by bounds without blocking maneuver, reserve, or sustainment routes.
- Maintain target acquisition, communications, survivability, and support-gap reporting.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Overall Concept

The group remains in general support throughout and changes priority with the corps main effort. It supports the 18th Division in Phases I–II, shifts to the 22nd Division for the Phase III attack on Velin, and returns to the 18th Division in Phase IV. The corps may temporarily redirect priority to the most dangerous counterattack, threatened retained objective, or committed 24th Mechanized Brigade. The group displaces by bounds to keep the current decisive task within range while preparing the next firing area.

#### (2) Phase I — Seize Aradesh and Clear to PL Punch

**Estimated Turns 1–6.** Provide priority of fires to the 18th Division attack on Aradesh, including the 14th Armored Brigade objective attack and the 15th Armored Brigade clearing operation south of the city. Give next priority to the 22nd Division movement to contact in the Maren Lowlands. Suppress or disrupt defenders, southern interference, reinforcement, and counterattack forces.

Displacement implication: cover Aradesh, the clearing area south of the city, and the Maren approaches while preparing routes and positions for the attack on Belas. The phase ends when Aradesh is seized, the area south of the city and PL Punch sectors are controlled, and Phase II is supportable.

#### (3) Phase II — Seize Belas and Retain Aradesh

**Estimated Turns 3–12.** Provide priority of fires to the 18th Division attack on Belas, especially the 15th Armored Brigade main effort. Give next priority to the 22nd Division fixing action against forces in Velin and near the Maren Bridge. Maintain coverage of the 23rd Mechanized Brigade retaining Aradesh.

Displacement implication: move to maintain range to Belas without abandoning supportability of Aradesh, the corps seam, and the most dangerous southern approach. The phase ends when Belas is seized, Aradesh remains secure, the fixing action is effective, and the Velin attack and southern guard are supportable.

#### (4) Phase III — Seize Velin and Guard the Aradesh Plain

**Estimated Turns 7–18.** Shift priority of fires to the 22nd Division attack on Velin, especially the 32nd Infantry Brigade. Maintain sufficient fires for the 18th Division guard, with temporary precedence to a counterattack moving north from the Maren Lowlands when corps directs or approved emergency criteria are met. Support the 15th and 23rd Brigades retaining Belas and Aradesh as capacity and range permit.

Displacement implication: position to support Velin while preserving a feasible shift against the southern counterattack and avoiding congestion on Maren routes. The phase ends when Velin is seized, PL Jab is controlled, Aradesh and Belas are retained, and Phase IV support is established.

#### (5) Phase IV — Guard and Retain

**Estimated Turns 15–20.** Return priority of fires to the 18th Division guard and the most dangerous counterattack; give next priority to the 22nd Division guard. Support the 14th Armored Brigade guard in the southern Aradesh Plain, the 24th Mechanized Brigade guard in the southern PL Jab sector, and the forces retaining all three cities according to corps priority.

Displacement implication: occupy survivable positions that cover the most dangerous approach, both guard sectors, retained objectives, and key routes while preserving an alternate displacement route.

### c. Scheme of Fires

Within the phase priority, target categories are:

1. Coalition artillery supporting the decisive defense or counterattack.
2. Armored or mechanized concentrations capable of defeating the current main effort or guard.
3. Defenders, reinforcements, or withdrawal forces affecting Aradesh, Belas, or Velin.
4. Forces moving north from the Maren Lowlands toward the Aradesh Plain.
5. Forces threatening retained objectives, the corps seam, maneuver routes, guard handovers, or a committed reserve.

The corps commander directs priority shifts and resolves competition among Aradesh, Belas, Velin, and the two guard sectors. The group commander recommends a shift when threat, range, route access, survivability, or supportability changes. A division request does not change priority unless the corps authenticates it or an approved emergency procedure applies.

### d. Movement and Survivability

- Occupy positions that range the current priority action and preserve a feasible displacement route.
- Reconnoiter and coordinate primary and alternate routes before movement.
- Avoid blocking the Aradesh–Belas routes, Maren routes, reserve routes, bridge approaches, casualty-evacuation routes, or sustainment traffic.
- Begin wet-weather displacement early enough to preserve next-phase support when practicable.
- Avoid unnecessary lateral movement between Belas and Velin directions during restricted weather.
- Displace when the current position loses required range, route access, survivability, or ability to support the next directed action.
- Report displacement start, estimated completion, coverage retained, and any support gap.

### e. Tasks to Organic Capabilities

- **Target acquisition — Task:** Locate coalition artillery, objective defenders, armored concentrations, reinforcement routes, and forces moving north from the Maren Lowlands. **Purpose:** Enable R-2F1 through R-2F4.
- **Liaison and communications — Task:** Maintain continuous coordination with corps and both divisions and authenticate priority changes. **Purpose:** Mass fires at the correct task and prevent conflicting support expectations.
- **Movement and survey — Task:** Prepare primary and alternate firing areas and displacement routes for central and southern actions. **Purpose:** Maintain range and reduce support gaps.
- **Sustainment and maintenance — Task:** Preserve ammunition, mobility, communications, and recovery capacity for the current and next phase. **Purpose:** Sustain fires through Turn 20.
- **Local protection — Task:** Maintain security and air-defense awareness sufficient to protect the aggregate formation. **Purpose:** Preserve the group’s combat capability.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Priority-Shift Triggers

- Corps directs the planned shift from 18th Division to 22nd Division in Phase III or the return to 18th Division in Phase IV.
- A concentrated coalition counterattack from the Maren Lowlands threatens the Aradesh Plain, retained objectives, or the Velin attack.
- The 24th Mechanized Brigade is committed during Phases I–III and corps assigns it fires priority.
- The current priority formation completes its supported task or no longer requires priority.
- Range, route, survivability, or ammunition status makes the current priority unsupportable; the group reports and recommends an alternative.

#### (2) Constraints and Risk Controls

- Remain in general support; division requests do not alter the support relationship or priority without corps authority.
- Do not promise full simultaneous support to separated Aradesh, Belas, Velin, guard, and reserve actions.
- Maintain enough flexibility during Phase III to respond to the southern counterattack without unintentionally abandoning the Velin priority.
- Confirm friendly locations before fires near seams, bridges, retains, handovers, populated areas, or uncertain front lines.
- Do not assume effects beyond Atlatl mechanics or agreed adjudication.
- Unresolved firing positions, routes, boundaries, ranges, and control measures remain `TBD—SCENARIO DESIGN`.

#### (3) CCIR

**PIR:** Coalition artillery; defenses and reinforcement routes at Aradesh, Belas, and Velin; counterattack formations moving north from the Maren Lowlands; threats to the corps seam or retained objectives; and enemy action forcing displacement.

**FFIR:** Unable to range or support the priority formation; communications lost with corps or supported formation; ammunition or mobility prevents the priority task; firing position compromised; displacement route blocked; route conflict delays support; or inability to support the current or next decisive action or guard.

#### (4) Reports

Report once per turn and on significant change: current priority formation and supported task; targets and effects; range and coverage; position and displacement status; primary and alternate route status; readiness, survivability, and ammunition concern; ability to support the current and next phase; and recommended priority shift. Immediately report forces moving north from the Maren Lowlands, all support gaps, priority conflicts, and FFIR.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept and Priorities

Sustainment preserves the group’s ability to shift among Aradesh, Belas, Velin, and both guard sectors without congesting corps routes. Priorities are ammunition for the current and next anticipated task; fuel, maintenance, and recovery for displacement; communications and target acquisition; local protection; and replenishment for the Phase IV guard.

### b. Transportation, Maintenance, and Medical Support

Coordinate artillery traffic with maneuver, reserve, bridge, and sustainment movement. Allow additional movement time during Turns 7–12 and protect route access between the central and southern sectors. Repair priority goes to capabilities preserving aggregate range, communications, mobility, and fires. Casualty evacuation follows the current secured support route.

### c. Atlatl Abstraction

Detailed ammunition, survey, target acquisition, maintenance, recovery, air defense, local security, and medical functions remain incorporated in the artillery counter and are adjudicated narratively.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains under 12th Maneuver Corps control in general support. The corps commander directs priority of fires and reserve support. The group commander recommends shifts and controls internal execution and displacement.

**Succession of command:** Group commander; deputy group commander; senior organic fires commander.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use corps fires and command networks; alternate and contingency means remain `TBD—SCENARIO DESIGN`. Priority changes, reserve commitment, counterattack warnings, displacement, and support gaps have precedence. Under degraded communications, continue the last authenticated priority, do not infer a reserve release, commitment, or priority shift, and avoid fires near uncertain friendly locations.
