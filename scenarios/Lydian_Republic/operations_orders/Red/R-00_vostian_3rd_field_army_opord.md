## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The field-army area of interest extends from the Vostian frontier west across eastern Lydia to the Arel River belt and the approaches to central Lydia. It includes the Drovna Heights, Kirov transportation hub, Aradesh Plain, Maren Lowlands, crossings near Kirov, Belas, and Velin, and the east-west and lateral routes connecting both corps.

The area of interest also includes Lydian and RDC Coalition reserve assembly areas and counterattack routes west of PL Jab, routes from the Maren Lowlands into the Aradesh Plain, and reinforcement routes from central Lydia. Political developments or coalition reinforcement could shorten the time available to seize and consolidate the field army’s objectives.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

- **Aradesh Plain:** Open terrain favors armored and mechanized concentration, penetration, and exploitation. Aradesh (`hex-12-6`) is the central political objective and anchors coalition defense east of the Arel.
- **Kirov transportation hub:** Kirov (`hex-5-3`) controls northern east-west and north-south routes. Its capture would disrupt coalition lateral movement and support a northern turn.
- **Drovna Heights:** Rough and wooded terrain restricts mechanized movement and observation. Infantry can clear or secure the secondary northern routes, but progress may be slower and more difficult to support.
- **Maren Lowlands:** Marshes and drainage features channel movement onto firm routes. This restricts maneuver but creates a southern route toward Velin and the coalition flank.
- **Arel River belt:** Crossings near Kirov, Belas (`hex-5-7`), and Velin (`hex-5-11`) are decisive. A usable crossing provides access to central Lydia and prevents the coalition from using the river as an uninterrupted defensive barrier.
- **Road network:** The field army’s tempo depends on controlling junctions, securing penetration shoulders, and preventing route conflicts between maneuver, artillery, and support counters.
- Exact boundaries, axes, start hexes, crossing hexes, phase lines, and support areas are displayed in the operational overlay.

### c. Enemy Forces

#### (1) Composition and Disposition

Vostian intelligence assesses that the RDC Coalition Land Corps controls two divisions, a corps-reserve armored brigade, division artillery, and corps fires. Lydian and RDC Coalition forces defend the Aradesh Plain, Drovna approaches, Maren Lowlands, key cities, and Arel crossings. Coalition armored formations are capable of local and corps-level counterattacks.

Coalition and Lydian forces hold Kirov, Aradesh, and the Arel crossings at scenario start. Their boundaries, passage plans, and commitment decisions remain the subject of continuing Vostian intelligence assessment.

#### (2) Capabilities and Limitations

The coalition can defend cities and restrictive terrain, exchange forward space for time, mass artillery against a penetration, shift mechanized forces through Kirov or Belas, and commit armored forces against an exposed Vostian flank. It may deny crossings by holding approaches and use the Arel River to shorten its defense.

Coalition forces are fewer, dispersed among separated objectives, politically constrained to defensive operations in Lydia, and dependent on a limited road and crossing network. Multinational coordination and the need to protect civilians and infrastructure may slow decisions. These limitations do not eliminate the danger of a concentrated armored counterattack.

#### (3) Most Likely Course of Action

The coalition defends Aradesh and Kirov with mechanized forces, uses light infantry to block the Drovna and Maren routes, retains local armored combat power behind the central sector, and holds its corps reserve west of the Arel until the Vostian main effort becomes clear. It trades nonessential eastern terrain for time, then counterattacks a penetration threatening a city or crossing.

#### (4) Most Dangerous Course of Action

The coalition deliberately permits a limited penetration, fixes the leading Vostian division near a restricted route or city, and masses armored forces and artillery against an exposed corps seam or penetration shoulder. A simultaneous counterattack from the Maren Lowlands into the Aradesh Plain could threaten Aradesh, Belas, the 12th Corps flank, and the field army’s east-west lines of communication.

### d. Friendly Forces

#### (1) Higher Strategic Direction and Intent

The Vostian government has directed the 3rd Field Army to displace Lydian and RDC Coalition forces from eastern Lydia, seize the principal administrative and transportation centers, secure the Arel bridge network, and establish defensible control east of PL Jab before coalition reinforcement changes the balance.

The strategic purpose is to convert Vostia’s temporary advantage in readiness and mass into a sustainable political and territorial outcome. The desired condition is control of Aradesh, Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, Belas, and Velin; a guarded western security line along PL Jab; secure lines of communication; and a mobile reserve prepared for follow-on action.

#### (2) Supporting Actors and Capabilities

Separatist organizations may provide local information, route knowledge, and limited support to control of facilities, but they are not playable counters and do not replace Vostian formations. Long-range fires, reconnaissance, aviation, air defense, engineering, electronic warfare, cyber, logistics, and medical support are incorporated into parent formations and do not create separate baseline actions or modifiers.

No additional playable reserve or follow-on formation exists beyond the approved OOB. The field-army-directed reserve is created by designating the organic 12th Armored Brigade during Phase IV; no new counter is created.

### e. Civil Considerations

Kirov and Aradesh contain divided populations, provisional separatist authorities, Lydian institutions, displaced civilians, and critical transport and energy infrastructure. The field army requires usable roads, bridges, administrative facilities, and communications nodes to consolidate its gains. Unnecessary destruction or uncontrolled civil disorder would undermine the political purpose and consume maneuver combat power. Civil-security activity is narrative and will not create new counters.

### f. Attachments and Detachments

None at scenario start. Cross-attachment between corps requires field-army direction. The base OOB remains authoritative.

### g. Assumptions

- Vostian forces retain initiative and local numerical superiority at scenario start.
- Kirov, Aradesh, and the Arel crossings remain under coalition/Lydian control and usable unless setup states otherwise.
- Additional coalition forces do not arrive as playable counters during the immediate scenario unless introduced by event.
- A corps may hold an organic brigade as its reserve without creating a new counter.
- Bridge status changes only through implemented Atlatl mechanics. Any optional bridge rule must be agreed before setup.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local, the **Vostian 3rd Field Army attacks west through PL Punch and PL Hook to seize OBJ Aradesh, OBJ Kirov, OBJ Bridge, OBJ Belas, and OBJ Velin; clear Lydian and RDC Coalition forces from the assigned area to PL Jab; and then guard along PL Jab while retaining the seized objectives in order to break the coalition defense in eastern Lydia, secure the Arel transportation and crossing network, and posture the field army for follow-on operations.**

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Overall Concept

The field army attacks west in four overlapping, conditions-based phases. The **12th Maneuver Corps is the field army main effort throughout the operation**. It seizes Aradesh in Phase I, Belas in Phase II, and Velin in Phase III, while retaining previously seized objectives and protecting its southern flank. The **7th Maneuver Corps is the supporting effort**. It conducts movement to contact and clearing actions in the northern sector, seizes Kirov and the Northwest Bridge, retains those objectives, and guards the northern portion of PL Jab.

The corps operate on mutually supporting axes. The 7th Corps develops and fixes coalition forces in the north so they cannot reinforce the 12th Corps sector. The 12th Corps seizes the field army’s successive central and southern objectives. Both corps secure their seam, routes, shoulders, and artillery displacement areas. A corps may begin the next phase when local conditions are met, but it remains responsible for existing retain, guard, seam, route, containment, and handover requirements until transfer is confirmed.

#### (2) Phase I — Seize Aradesh and Advance to PL Punch

**Estimated Turns 1–6.** The **12th Maneuver Corps**, as the field army main effort, attacks to seize OBJ Aradesh and clears its assigned area through PL Punch. It destroys or displaces coalition forces in the Aradesh Plain and Maren Lowlands that can prevent seizure of Aradesh or interfere with follow-on operations.

The **7th Maneuver Corps** conducts a movement to contact through the northern Aradesh Plain and Drovna Heights to PL Punch to establish contact and develop the enemy situation. On contact, it fixes identified coalition forces and clears assigned terrain to prevent reinforcement of Aradesh and protect the main effort’s northern flank.

The phase ends when Aradesh is seized, the 12th Corps controls the routes required for Phase II, both corps have secured their required portions of PL Punch, and attacks toward Kirov, OBJ Bridge, and Belas are supportable.

#### (3) Phase II — Seize OBJ Bridge, Kirov, and Belas

**Estimated Turns 3–12.** The **12th Maneuver Corps** remains the field army main effort. It attacks to seize OBJ Belas, clears its assigned area to PL Hook, and retains Aradesh. It fixes coalition forces in and around Velin and the Maren Bridge long enough to prevent them from interfering with the attack on Belas.

The **7th Maneuver Corps** attacks to seize OBJ Kirov and OBJ Bridge and clears its assigned area to PL Hook. It prevents coalition forces in the Drovna Heights or northern Arel corridor from moving against the 12th Corps.

The phase ends when Kirov, OBJ Bridge, and Belas are seized; Aradesh remains under Vostian control; routes and crossings needed for Phase III are usable; and both corps can continue toward PL Jab without exposing the field army seam or a previously seized objective.

#### (4) Phase III — Seize Velin and Advance to PL Jab

**Estimated Turns 7–18.** The **12th Maneuver Corps** remains the field army main effort. It attacks to seize OBJ Velin and clears its assigned area to PL Jab. The 18th Armored Division retains Aradesh and Belas and conducts a guard on the southern end of the Aradesh Plain. The **14th Armored Brigade performs the guard mission to identify and delay counterattacking forces moving north from the Maren Lowlands into the Aradesh Plain**, thereby protecting the 22nd Mechanized Division’s attack on Velin and the 12th Corps lines of communication. The 22nd Mechanized Division attacks to seize Velin. The 24th Mechanized Brigade remains the 12th Corps reserve.

The **7th Maneuver Corps** conducts a movement to contact to PL Jab to establish or regain contact and develop the enemy situation. On contact, it fixes and destroys coalition forces that threaten the field army’s northern flank or retained objectives. It retains Kirov and the Northwest Bridge.

The phase ends when Velin is seized, the designated sectors of PL Jab are controlled, all previously seized objectives remain under Vostian control, and the corps have established guard, retain, reserve, artillery, and sustainment postures for Phase IV.

#### (5) Phase IV — Guard, Consolidate, and Prepare for Follow-on Operations

**Estimated Turns 15–20.** The **12th Maneuver Corps** remains the field army main effort. It conducts a guard along its assigned portion of PL Jab to provide early warning, prevent enemy ground observation and direct fire against the consolidating main body, and fight to gain time. The 18th Armored Division is the 12th Corps main effort; it guards the southern Aradesh Plain and retains Aradesh and Belas. The 22nd Mechanized Division guards the southern portion of PL Jab; the **24th Mechanized Brigade is released from corps reserve to conduct the guard while the 32nd Infantry Brigade retains Velin**.

The **7th Maneuver Corps** conducts a guard along its assigned portion of PL Jab and retains Kirov and the Northwest Bridge. The **12th Armored Brigade transitions from corps reserve to field-army-directed reserve** and remains available for commitment against the most dangerous counterattack or for follow-on action.

The phase ends at scenario completion or on receipt of a new order.

### c. Scheme of Maneuver

The 12th Corps attacks along the central and southern corridors. It concentrates combat power successively against Aradesh, Belas, and Velin while assigning forces to retain each objective after seizure. The 18th Armored Division leads the corps through Phase II, then retains Aradesh and Belas and protects the corps from a counterattack out of the Maren Lowlands. The 22nd Mechanized Division fixes coalition forces around Velin during Phase II, attacks to seize Velin during Phase III, and guards the southern sector during Phase IV.

The 7th Corps advances through the northern Aradesh Plain and Drovna Heights. It uses movement to contact when enemy dispositions are unclear, transitions to attack when sufficient contact is established, seizes Kirov and the Northwest Bridge, and then advances to and guards PL Jab. The 11th Armored Division is the corps main effort; the 14th Mechanized Division protects the Drovna flank and seizes and retains the Northwest Bridge.

The field army will not continue west beyond supporting range merely to gain distance. A corps that bypasses enemy forces or urban resistance assigns containment, route-security, shoulder, and handover responsibilities. Boundaries, routes, objectives, and phase lines will be adjusted only by authenticated order or approved overlay.

### d. Scheme of Fires

Each corps artillery group remains under parent-corps control in general support. Field army priorities of fires are:

1. **Phase I:** 12th Corps seizure of Aradesh; next priority to 7th Corps movement to contact and fixing actions.
2. **Phase II:** 12th Corps seizure of Belas; next priority to 7th Corps seizure of Kirov and OBJ Bridge.
3. **Phase III:** 12th Corps seizure of Velin and protection from counterattack out of the Maren Lowlands; next priority to 7th Corps movement to contact to PL Jab.
4. **Phase IV:** Defeat of the most dangerous counterattack against the 12th Corps main effort, followed by support to the 7th Corps guard and field-army-directed reserve.

Within the 7th Corps, the 7th Corps Artillery Group gives priority of fires to the 11th Armored Division and next priority to the 14th Mechanized Division. Within the 12th Corps, the 12th Corps Artillery Group gives priority to the 18th Armored Division during Phases I–II, shifts priority to the 22nd Mechanized Division during Phase III, and returns priority to the 18th Armored Division during Phase IV.

Fires support movement to contact, suppress and destroy identified enemy systems, isolate objectives, prevent reinforcement, disrupt counterattack formations, protect bridges and routes, and cover artillery and reserve displacement. A main-effort designation does not eliminate minimum support to a formation retaining a city, bridge, route, or corps flank.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Effective Time and Phase Windows

This order is effective on receipt. Phase I begins 18 May 2030 at 0600 local. Planning windows are Phase I, Turns 1–6; Phase II, Turns 3–12; Phase III, Turns 7–18; and Phase IV, Turns 15–20. Windows may overlap. Conditions and authenticated decisions govern transition.

#### (2) Control Measures

- Phase lines, east to west: PL Punch, PL Hook, and PL Jab.
- Named objectives: OBJ Aradesh, OBJ Kirov, OBJ Bridge at the Northwest Bridge, OBJ Belas, and OBJ Velin.
- Known locations: Kirov `hex-5-3`; Belas `hex-5-7`; Velin `hex-5-11`; Aradesh `hex-12-6`.
- Corps coordinate directly at the central seam and report any route conflict, uncovered gap, or enemy force capable of crossing the seam.
- No formation crosses a corps boundary, changes a named objective, or advances beyond an assigned phase line without authority from the establishing headquarters.
- A formation retains responsibility for a city, bridge, route, seam, shoulder, or contained enemy force until the receiving formation confirms handover.

#### (3) Commander’s Critical Information Requirements

**PIR**

1. Location, composition, and direction of coalition armored reserves and counterattack forces.
2. Coalition intent at Aradesh, Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, Belas, Velin, and PL Jab.
3. Coalition forces moving from the Maren Lowlands toward the Aradesh Plain.
4. Usability and control of the Northwest Bridge, Kirov approaches, Belas corridor, Maren Bridge, and Velin routes.
5. Coalition forces capable of attacking a corps seam, artillery group, route, or retained objective.
6. Evidence that coalition forces are withdrawing west of PL Jab or preparing a coordinated counterattack.

**FFIR**

1. Any Vostian maneuver formation at or below 70 strength, isolated, unable to move, or unable to perform its essential task.
2. Loss or blockage of a route required by the 12th Corps main effort.
3. Loss, isolation, or threatened loss of Aradesh, Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, Belas, or Velin.
4. Either corps artillery group unable to support its priority formation.
5. The 12th Corps unable to protect the Aradesh Plain from a counterattack out of the Maren Lowlands.
6. The 12th Armored Brigade unavailable to perform the field-army reserve mission during Phase IV.
7. A corps advancing beyond supporting range of its artillery, sustainment, flank security, or adjacent corps.

#### (5) Movement to Contact and Actions on Contact

- Units conduct movement to contact when enemy dispositions are insufficiently clear for an attack.
- The purpose of movement to contact is to establish or regain contact and develop the situation. Units make contact with the smallest suitable force while preserving combat power to act on contact.
- On contact, commanders report the enemy, develop the situation, establish local security, and select the appropriate action: attack, defend, bypass, delay, or withdraw within their authority and higher purpose.
- A movement-to-contact mission does not authorize abandonment of a phase line, retain, seam, route, guard, or supporting responsibility.
- Identified enemy forces may be fixed to prevent movement, cleared from an assigned area, or destroyed when required by the higher mission and supported by available combat power.

#### (6) Reserve Employment

- A reserve remains withheld from the initial action and receives planning priorities rather than simultaneous committed missions.
- The 12th Armored Brigade remains organic to the 11th Armored Division. The 7th Corps controls positioning and readiness until the brigade becomes the field-army-directed reserve; field army commitment authority does not permanently detach it.
- The 24th Mechanized Brigade remains organic to the 22nd Mechanized Division while serving as the 12th Corps reserve. It begins the Phase IV guard only after corps release.
- A commitment order specifies task and purpose, supported force, route, objective or area, limit of advance, fires and sustainment support, termination condition, and follow-on posture.

#### (7) Rules, Constraints, and Risk Controls

- Preserve usable bridges, roads, rail, energy, and administrative infrastructure where operationally practicable.
- Do not disperse armored brigades to secure routine occupied terrain when another formation can perform the task.
- Prevent fires or movement across corps seams without coordination and positive identification.
- Do not bypass a city or enemy force unless containment, route-security, shoulder, and handover responsibilities are assigned.
- Do not continue west beyond the assigned phase line or supporting range merely to gain distance.
- A corps may begin an adjacent-phase task when conditions are met, but it retains existing responsibilities until handover is confirmed.
- A unit conducting guard identifies the protected force or activity, guard sector, observation and reporting requirements, engagement criteria, delay expectations, disengagement criteria, and rearward handover.

#### (8) Required Reports

- Contact report after each new engagement or significant change in enemy disposition.
- Strength and mission-capability report at each turn end and immediately at an FFIR threshold.
- Objective, bridge, route, seam, phase-line, and retain-status report whenever control or usability changes.
- Reserve readiness and location each turn until commitment or release.
- Coalition reserve or counterattack-force sighting immediately.
- Corps artillery priority, displacement status, range, and ability to support the current and next phase.
- Phase-transition recommendation with supporting objective, enemy, route, fires, sustainment, and flank information.
- Any conflict between this order, Annex C, an overlay, or a later authenticated order. The later authenticated order governs; report the conflict and request clarification.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept of Support

Field-army sustainment flows from Vostia along separate northern and central-southern routes. Support follows the 12th Corps main effort while preserving the 7th Corps ability to seize and retain its objectives and guard the northern flank. The field army builds distribution depth before the wet period and will not place both corps’ critical support on one vulnerable route.

### b. Priorities of Support

1. The field-army main effort.
2. The division or brigade executing the decisive task within the 12th Corps.
3. The corps or division defeating a decisive coalition counterattack.
4. Corps artillery supporting the decisive operation.
5. Formations securing a city, route, or bridgehead essential to the end state.
6. The 12th Armored Brigade when preparing for or executing the field-army reserve mission.

### c. Supply and Transportation

Fuel priority supports successive armored attacks, artillery displacement, and reserve commitment. Ammunition priority supports seizure of the current phase objective, corps artillery, and defeat of coalition counterattack. Corps identify primary and alternate routes where the final map permits and report route loss immediately. Movement priority follows the 12th Corps main effort unless changed by authenticated order.

### d. Maintenance and Recovery

Priority is the 12th Corps decisive formation, corps artillery, formations retaining decisive objectives, and the field-army reserve. A formation approaching the Atlatl removal threshold will not be expended solely to hold nonessential terrain when consolidation or relief preserves the higher mission.

### e. Medical and Casualty Evacuation

Casualty evacuation moves east along secured lines of communication. When a route is cut, the affected corps establishes an alternate through its own or the adjacent corps area. Detailed medical functions are incorporated into parent formations and provide planning context only in baseline play.

### f. Personnel, Detainees, and Civilians

Corps report displaced populations, detainee concentrations, or local-security demands that obstruct decisive routes or consume maneuver capacity. Separatist and provisional authorities may assist narratively, but maneuver counters will not be diverted into unsupported civil-security mechanics.

### g. Atlatl Abstraction

Headquarters, supply, medical, engineers, reconnaissance, air defense, aviation, airpower, cyber/EW, and detailed recovery are not separate counters. These functions guide priorities but create no additional baseline game actions or modifiers.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

#### (1) Command Relationships

- The Vostian 3rd Field Army exercises operational control of the 7th and 12th Maneuver Corps.
- Each corps retains operational control of its two divisions; its artillery group remains in general support.
- During Phase IV, the 7th Corps holds the 12th Armored Brigade as the field-army-directed reserve while it remains organic to the 11th Armored Division.
- Cross-attachment between corps requires field-army direction.

#### (2) Command Posts and Commander Location

The field-army main command post remains in the eastern Vostian support area at a location confirmed during scenario setup. The tactical command element positions to maintain communications with both corps and control the decisive operation without blocking a main-effort route.

#### (3) Succession of Command

1. Commander, Vostian 3rd Field Army.
2. Field-army deputy commander.
3. Commander, 12th Maneuver Corps.
4. Commander, 7th Maneuver Corps.

#### (4) Liaison

Both corps maintain liaison with the field army and each other. Liaison reports seam status, artillery range and priority, reserve readiness, route conflicts, objective handovers, guard-sector coordination, and support requirements. Any command or interoperability restriction affecting the mission is reported immediately.

### b. Signal

- Primary communications use the Vostian field-army tactical network; alternate means use corps networks and liaison relays; contingency means use scheduled reports, messenger, and prearranged actions.
- Phase transitions, changes to the field army main effort, commitment of the field-army-directed reserve, and changes in corps boundaries or objectives require authenticated field-army direction.
- Objective, counterattack, guard, reserve, bridge, route, artillery-supportability, and FFIR reports have precedence.
- Under degraded communications, corps continue within the last confirmed mission and phase. They will not assume a main-effort shift, cross a corps boundary, or commit the field-army-directed reserve without authenticated direction, but may act to preserve their formations or defeat an immediate threat within their assigned area.
