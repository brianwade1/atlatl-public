# 7th Corps Artillery Group

## OPORD R-1F — OPERATION NORTHERN FIRES

> **FOR WARGAMING AND EDUCATIONAL USE ONLY — ENTIRELY FICTIONAL**

- **Issuing headquarters:** 7th Corps Artillery Group
- **Date-time group:** 18 May 2030, 0600 local
- **Effective:** On receipt
- **Duration:** 20 turns / 10 days; 12 hours per turn
- **Parent order:** 7th Maneuver Corps OPORD R-10, OPERATION NORTHERN HAMMER
- **Support relationship:** General support to 7th Maneuver Corps
- **Supported parent tasks:** R-1C1 through R-1C6

### Task Organization

The group is represented by one aggregate artillery formation in Atlatl. Command, target acquisition, communications, survey, sustainment, maintenance, recovery, medical, air-defense awareness, and local security remain internal. No subordinate batteries, calibers, or ammunition quantities are separately represented.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Operations

The group supports operations from the northern Aradesh Plain through the Drovna Heights, Kirov, OBJ Bridge, and the 7th Corps portion of PL Jab. Open terrain favors observation but exposes firing positions. Rough terrain and limited routes in Drovna restrict movement and range. Kirov, bridge approaches, division seams, and routes used by maneuver, reserve, sustainment, and artillery movement require close coordination.

The Atlatl artillery counter has a maximum attack range of two hexes. Named support areas are broad maneuver areas rather than fixed hexes: the eastern corps support area during Phase I; the eastern Kirov and Northwest Bridge approaches during Phase II; secured objective areas and forward routes during Phase III; and positions supporting the northern PL Jab guard during Phase IV.

At setup and before each displacement, the Red player selects a legal firing hex and route that keep the current priority action within two hexes, remain inside the 7th Corps sector, preserve a feasible displacement route, and avoid blocking maneuver, reserve, bridge, or sustainment movement. Target-reference points are planning aids and create no separate engine effect.

### b. Weather and Light

The R-10 forecast applies. Dry conditions through Turn 6 favor observation and displacement. Rain during Turns 7–12 increases dependence on firm routes, slows movement and resupply, and creates the greatest risk of artillery displacement conflicting with maneuver traffic. Fog can conceal movement but delay target acquisition. Weather effects not native to Atlatl provide planning context only and cause no additional baseline game modifier.

### c. Enemy Forces

Lydian and RDC Coalition artillery and maneuver formations can defend Kirov and OBJ Bridge, reinforce across northern routes, counterattack an exposed seam or retained objective, and strike artillery positions or displacement routes. The most dangerous enemy action is a coordinated counterattack against Kirov, the Northwest Bridge, or the corps seam while the group is displaced or out of range.

### d. Friendly Forces

- **7th Maneuver Corps:** Conducts movement to contact to PL Punch; seizes Kirov and OBJ Bridge; conducts movement to contact to PL Jab; then guards and retains both objectives.
- **11th Armored Division:** Corps main effort throughout. The 11th Armored Brigade leads maneuver; the 13th Mechanized Brigade retains Kirov in Phases III–IV; the 12th Armored Brigade is corps reserve in Phases I–III and field-army-directed reserve in Phase IV.
- **14th Mechanized Division:** Supporting effort. The 21st Mechanized Brigade leads maneuver and the 31st Infantry Brigade retains the Northwest Bridge in Phases III–IV.

### e. Civil Considerations

Preserve Kirov’s essential road network, the Northwest Bridge, and routes required for follow-on movement when consistent with the mission. Confirm target and friendly locations before fires near cities, bridges, seams, handovers, or uncertain front lines.

### f. Assumptions

- The group remains one aggregate counter and cannot provide full simultaneous support to separated actions.
- Range, target eligibility, movement, and combat effects follow Atlatl mechanics; unmodeled effects have no baseline game effect.
- The corps commander authenticates changes in priority of fires.
- A phase transition does not by itself change fires priority or reserve authority.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 18 May 2030 at 0600 local, the **7th Corps Artillery Group provides general support to the 7th Maneuver Corps; gives priority of fires to the 11th Armored Division and next priority to the 14th Mechanized Division; shifts temporarily on corps order to the most dangerous counterattack or committed reserve; and displaces to maintain support through PL Jab in order to enable seizure and retention of Kirov and OBJ Bridge, protect the field army northern flank, and preserve corps freedom of action.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander’s Intent

#### Purpose

Concentrate the corps’ limited artillery effects at the action that creates or preserves the greatest northern operational advantage while maintaining enough coverage to protect the supporting division, retained objectives, and corps seam.

#### Key Tasks

- Remain in general support under corps control.
- Maintain priority of fires to the 11th Armored Division unless the corps directs a temporary shift.
- Suppress or disrupt forces opposing the current decisive action.
- Disrupt reinforcement and counterattack forces threatening Kirov, OBJ Bridge, the corps seam, or the field army main effort.
- Displace by bounds to maintain range to the current and next phase without blocking maneuver or reserve routes.
- Maintain target acquisition, communications, survivability, and support-gap reporting.
- Preserve sufficient capability for the Phase IV guard and any field-army-directed reserve commitment.

#### End State

- The current corps decisive action receives timely artillery support.
- Kirov, OBJ Bridge, the corps seam, and critical routes are not lost for lack of an identified or reported support limitation.
- The group remains survivable, mobile, and able to support the guard through Turn 20.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Overall Concept

The group remains in general support throughout. Priority of fires stays with the 11th Armored Division and next priority with the 14th Mechanized Division. The corps commander may temporarily shift priority to a dangerous counterattack, threatened retained objective, or committed reserve. The group displaces by bounds so the current priority action remains supported while the next firing area is prepared.

#### (2) Phase I — Clear to PL Punch

**Estimated Turns 1–6.** Provide priority of fires to the 11th Division clearing attack in the northern Aradesh Plain, especially the 11th Armored Brigade main effort. Give next priority to the 14th Division movement to contact in the Drovna Heights. Suppress or disrupt forces blocking movement, reinforcing Aradesh, or threatening the corps seam.

Displacement implication: remain positioned to support both division sectors through PL Punch and prepare routes and positions for Phase II. The phase ends when both sectors control PL Punch and the attacks on Kirov and OBJ Bridge are supportable.

#### (3) Phase II — Seize Kirov and OBJ Bridge

**Estimated Turns 3–12.** Provide priority of fires to the 11th Division attack on Kirov and next priority to the 14th Division attack on OBJ Bridge. Suppress objective defenders, disrupt reinforcement and withdrawal, and protect the division seam and objective approaches.

Displacement implication: move only when the group can preserve support to the current objective attack or clearly report the accepted support gap. The phase ends when both objectives are seized, approaches are secure, and Phase III movement is supportable.

#### (4) Phase III — Movement to Contact to PL Jab

**Estimated Turns 7–18.** Provide priority of fires to the 11th Division movement to contact and next priority to the 14th Division movement to contact. Temporarily shift on corps direction to defeat the most dangerous threat to Kirov, OBJ Bridge, the corps seam, or a committed 12th Armored Brigade.

Displacement implication: move forward by bounds to keep PL Jab, the division routes, and retained objectives within supportable range. The phase ends when PL Jab is controlled, both objectives are retained, and the group can support the Phase IV guard.

#### (5) Phase IV — Guard and Retain

**Estimated Turns 15–20.** Provide priority of fires to the 11th Division guard and any field-army-directed commitment of the 12th Armored Brigade; give next priority to the 14th Division guard. Disrupt counterattack formations before they reach the guard forces and support disengagement or handover as directed.

Displacement implication: occupy survivable positions that cover the most dangerous counterattack approach, Kirov, OBJ Bridge, and the reserve route while preserving at least one alternate route.

### c. Scheme of Fires

Within the phase priority, target categories are:

1. Coalition artillery supporting the decisive defense or counterattack.
2. Armored or mechanized concentrations capable of defeating the current main effort or guard.
3. Forces reinforcing, withdrawing from, or counterattacking Kirov or OBJ Bridge.
4. Forces threatening the corps seam, division routes, retaining brigades, or committed reserve.
5. Other targets whose engagement directly enables the supported formation’s assigned task and purpose.

The corps commander directs priority shifts. The group commander recommends a shift when threat, range, route access, survivability, or supportability changes. A division request does not change priority unless the corps authenticates it or an approved emergency procedure applies.

### d. Movement and Survivability

- Occupy positions that range the current priority action and preserve a feasible displacement route.
- Reconnoiter and coordinate primary and alternate routes before movement.
- Avoid blocking maneuver, reserve, bridge, casualty-evacuation, or sustainment routes.
- Begin wet-weather displacement early enough to preserve next-phase support when practicable.
- Displace when the current position loses required range, route access, survivability, or ability to support the next directed action.
- Report displacement start, estimated completion, coverage retained, and any support gap.
- Use movement and timely displacement to reduce vulnerability. Detailed dispersion, local security, survey, and technical survivability measures remain internal to the aggregate counter; the player represents their operational effect through legal counter movement and positioning.

### e. Tasks to Organic Capabilities

- **Target acquisition — Task:** Locate coalition artillery, objective defenders, armored concentrations, reinforcement routes, and threats to seams and guard forces. **Purpose:** Enable R-1F1 through R-1F4.
- **Liaison and communications — Task:** Maintain continuous coordination with corps and both divisions and authenticate priority changes. **Purpose:** Mass fires at the correct task and prevent conflicting support expectations.
- **Movement and survey — Task:** Prepare primary and alternate firing areas and displacement routes. **Purpose:** Maintain range and reduce support gaps.
- **Sustainment and maintenance — Task:** Preserve ammunition, mobility, communications, and recovery capacity for the current and next phase. **Purpose:** Sustain fires through Turn 20.
- **Local protection — Task:** Maintain security and air-defense awareness sufficient to protect the aggregate formation. **Purpose:** Preserve the group’s combat capability.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Priority-Shift Triggers

- Corps directs a phase priority change.
- A concentrated coalition counterattack threatens Kirov, OBJ Bridge, the corps seam, or the current main effort.
- The 12th Armored Brigade is committed by proper authority and corps assigns it fires priority.
- The current priority formation completes its supported task or no longer requires priority.
- Range, route, survivability, or ammunition status makes the current priority unsupportable; the group reports and recommends an alternative.

#### (2) Constraints and Risk Controls

- Remain in general support; division requests do not alter the support relationship or priority without corps authority.
- Do not promise full simultaneous support to separated Kirov, OBJ Bridge, PL Jab, and reserve actions.
- Confirm friendly locations before fires near seams, passages, bridges, handovers, populated areas, or uncertain front lines.
- Do not assume effects beyond Atlatl mechanics. Any optional house rule or controller inject must be agreed before play.
- The approved operational graphics govern depicted corps boundaries, objectives, phase lines, and guard sectors. Firing hexes and displacement routes remain player decisions under Paragraphs 1.a and 3.d; no additional fires control measure is required for baseline play.

#### (3) CCIR

**PIR:** Coalition artillery; counterattack formations; reinforcement routes to Kirov or OBJ Bridge; threats to the corps seam or retained objectives; and enemy action forcing displacement.

**FFIR:** Unable to range or support the priority formation; communications lost with corps or supported formation; ammunition or mobility prevents the priority task; firing position compromised; displacement route blocked; route conflict delays support; or inability to support the current or next decisive action.

#### (4) Reports

Report once per turn and on significant change: current priority formation and supported task; targets and effects; range and coverage; position and displacement status; primary and alternate route status; readiness, survivability, and ammunition concern; ability to support the current and next phase; and recommended priority shift. Report all support gaps and FFIR immediately.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept and Priorities

Sustainment preserves the group’s ability to shift between the 11th and 14th Divisions without congesting corps routes. Priorities are ammunition for the current and next anticipated task; fuel, maintenance, and recovery for displacement; communications and target acquisition; local protection; and replenishment for the Phase IV guard.

### b. Transportation, Maintenance, and Medical Support

Coordinate artillery traffic with maneuver, reserve, bridge, and sustainment movement. Allow additional movement time during Turns 7–12. Repair priority goes to capabilities preserving aggregate range, communications, mobility, and fires. Casualty evacuation follows the current secured support route.

### c. Atlatl Abstraction

Detailed ammunition, survey, target acquisition, maintenance, recovery, air defense, local security, and medical functions remain incorporated in the artillery counter. They provide planning context but create no additional baseline game action or modifier.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains under 7th Maneuver Corps control in general support. The corps commander directs priority of fires and reserve support. The group commander recommends shifts and controls internal execution and displacement.

**Succession of command:** Group commander; deputy group commander; senior organic fires commander.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use 7th Corps fires and command networks. Alternate means use supported-division networks and liaison, with field-army or adjacent-corps relay coordinated through corps headquarters. Contingency means use prearranged report schedules, unit relay, and messenger. Priority changes, reserve commitment, counterattack warnings, displacement, and support gaps have precedence. Under degraded communications, continue the last authenticated priority, do not infer a reserve commitment or priority shift, and avoid fires near uncertain friendly locations.
