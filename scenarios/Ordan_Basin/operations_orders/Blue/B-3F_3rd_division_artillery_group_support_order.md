### Task Organization

The group is represented as one aggregate artillery formation with an initial Atlatl strength of 100. Organic command, target acquisition, survey, communications, local security, air defense, sustainment, maintenance, recovery, and medical functions remain incorporated in the counter. No attachments or detachments are in effect at scenario start.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The area of interest includes Red assembly and artillery areas east of OBJ North, Dalen, Central Link Bridge, and OBJ South; the Saren Heights and northern bypass approaches; Kasar and routes affecting PP South; the seam with the 1st Ordan Mechanized Division; and the northern and central Phase V battle-handover areas. It includes threats capable of interdicting the 6th Division's passage or severing its line of communication through OBJ South.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

The Arven River and its crossing network dominate the division area. North Pass Bridge, Dalen, Central Link Bridge, OBJ South, and the PP South route are separate operational responsibilities. The Saren Heights restrict movement and observation while channeling Red forces toward the northern crossing. Open basin terrain east of Dalen and OBJ South favors observation and long-range fires but exposes firing positions to counterfire.

PP South and Route Blue must simultaneously support the 6th Division's passage, artillery displacement, recovery, sustainment, and the 3rd Division's crossing defense. Congestion or loss of the route can interrupt fires and isolate forces east of the river. The group must protect the passage without abandoning the northern and central crossings; as one aggregate Atlatl formation, it cannot provide full simultaneous support to widely separated actions.

#### (2) Weather and Light

Plan for early-autumn conditions with cool mornings, generally trafficable ground, variable cloud cover, and possible fog or short rain periods. Darkness occurs during each night turn. Fog, rain, smoke, or darkness may degrade observation. Wet conditions increase dependence on roads and bridges and may delay ammunition movement or displacement. Weather effects not represented in Atlatl require consistent scenario-control adjudication.

### c. Enemy Forces

The Karsovian 4th Maneuver Corps can contest the northern and central crossings, mass artillery against PP South, and counterattack from the Kasar area. Red may use the 5th Guards Armored Brigade to strike the passage, exploit a lost crossing, or threaten the 6th Division's line of communication. High-value threats are Red artillery engaging PP South, maneuver formations capable of reaching a crossing or passage lane, concentrations in the Saren approaches, forces threatening the division seam, and units pursuing the Phase V guard.

Red's most likely course is to pressure the crossing network while using artillery to disrupt passage and sustainment. Its most dangerous course is a coordinated artillery and armored attack against OBJ South during the 6th Division passage, supported by a diversion in the north, designed to trap the passing division east of the Arven.

### d. Friendly Forces

The 3rd Compact Armored Division is the corps main effort in Phase I. The 31st Coalition Armored Brigade seizes OBJ South and opens PP South; the 32nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade seizes OBJ North and North Pass Bridge; and the 33rd Coalition Mechanized Brigade seizes Dalen and Central Link Bridge. The division retains the crossing network through Phase V.

In Phase II, the 3rd Division is the stationary force and controls PP South while the 6th Compact Mechanized Division passes. The 6th Division assumes responsibility east of the BHL when its lead brigade crosses, communications and fires are established, and both division commanders confirm handover. The 3rd Division retains the bridge and western approach. In Phase V, the division receives withdrawing guard elements, principally the 63rd Brigade north of PL Bronze and any designated central elements, then assumes responsibility for the Red force entering its engagement area.

The 1st Ordan Mechanized Division defends south of the division boundary. Its artillery group protects the southern crossing defense and receives the 62nd Brigade's guard fight.

### e. Civil Considerations

Dalen, bridge approaches, settlements near the crossing network, and the road system contain civilians and critical transport infrastructure. Fires require positive identification, especially during passage and battle handover. Preserve bridges and essential infrastructure unless destruction is authorized to prevent a greater operational loss.

### f. Assumptions

- No separate observer, radar, air, ammunition, or sustainment counters are created.
- Exact firing areas, range, target eligibility, ammunition effects, and combat results follow Atlatl and scenario-control adjudication.
- Cross-boundary or reinforcing fires support the 6th Division only on corps or division order and do not change the group's general-support relationship.
- The 6th Division Artillery Group will establish liaison and continuous-coverage coordination before its first artillery echelon enters PP South.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 12 September 2030 at 0600 local, the **3rd Division Artillery Group provides general support to the 3rd Compact Armored Division, suppresses and obscures Red crossing defenses, conducts counterfire, protects PP South and the 6th Division's forward passage, disrupts Red mass against the retained crossings, and supports Phase V guard disengagement and battle handover in order to enable seizure and retention of the northern and central Arven crossing network and preserve the corps line of communication.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander's Intent

**Purpose:** Preserve continuous, massed, and survivable fires across the crossing network while enabling the corps main effort to pass through PP South.

**Key tasks:** Support seizure of every assigned crossing; give decisive Phase I support to OBJ South; protect PP South and the passage lane; coordinate continuous coverage with 6th Division Artillery; retain the ability to defeat Red mass against a crossing; support the 63rd Brigade's Phase V disengagement north of PL Bronze; transfer fires without a gap at battle handover; preserve mobility and ammunition.

**End state:** The crossing network and PP South remain under Blue control; the 6th Division completes its passage without an artillery-coverage gap; Red cannot mass effectively against an assigned crossing; guard elements disengage in order; the fight transfers to the crossing defense; and the group remains capable through scenario end.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Phase I - Seize the Crossing Network

Provide general support with priority to the 31st, then 33rd, then 32nd Brigade. Suppress and obscure crossing defenses, conduct counterfire, and protect the approaches to OBJ South, Dalen/Central Link Bridge, and OBJ North/North Pass Bridge. Mass first on the division decisive operation at OBJ South while maintaining responsive support to the other seizure forces. This phase ends when all assigned crossing sites are controlled, OBJ South and PP South are trafficable and secure, passage control is established, and the 6th Division is ready to pass.

#### (2) Phase II - Protect PP South and Facilitate Passage

Protect PP South, the passage lane, and the retained crossings. Coordinate target responsibilities, boundaries, communications, displacement timing, and continuous coverage with 6th Division Artillery. On corps order, provide reinforcing fires to planned 6th Division targets when immediate crossing-defense requirements remain adequately supported. The group retains responsibility for protection of PP South and the western approach throughout passage. This phase ends for the passage-support surge when the designated 6th Division rear element clears PP South and both divisions confirm completion; crossing-defense tasks continue.

#### (3) Phase III - Retain During the Ruda Attack

Give priority to the assigned crossing under the most immediate threat. Conduct counterfire, disrupt Red concentrations, protect PP South and the division seam, and execute planned cross-boundary fires on corps order. Preserve positions and ammunition needed for Phase V reception. This phase ends on corps transition to Phase IV.

#### (4) Phase IV - Retain and Prepare Guard Reception

Continue the crossing defense. Prepare liaison, targets, routes, recognition, fire-control measures, and reception fires for withdrawing 6th Division guard forces. Coordinate with 6th Division Artillery so its westward echeloning does not block PP South or create a coverage gap. This phase ends when Guard Line Steel and PL Bronze are effective, the division reception plan is ready, and corps authorizes Phase V.

#### (5) Phase V - Support the Northern and Central Corps Defense

Give priority first to the 63rd Brigade or other designated guard element in contact or disengaging into the division sector. Suppress Red forces threatening withdrawal, obscure designated routes when directed, and protect the battle-handover area. After guard elements clear the BHL, the 3rd Division acknowledges responsibility, and fire coordination is transferred, shift priority to the crossing threatened by the Red main body. Continue to protect PP South as a corps line of communication and withdrawal route. This phase continues through scenario end or subsequent order.

### c. Scheme of Fires

#### (1) Priority Hierarchy

1. **Phase I:** 31st, 33rd, then 32nd Brigade.
2. **Phase II:** PP South and immediate crossing defense; then corps-directed reinforcing fires to 6th Division planned targets.
3. **Phases III-IV:** The assigned crossing under the most immediate threat; then planned cross-boundary targets on corps order.
4. **Phase V before battle handover:** The 63rd Brigade or designated northern/central guard element in contact or disengaging.
5. **Phase V after battle handover:** The crossing threatened by the Red main body, with PP South protected continuously.

The division commander directs priority shifts. The group recommends a shift whenever enemy threat, passage status, supported brigade capability, range, route access, or survivability changes. Priority to the passage or a guard element does not remove the group's responsibility to preserve the crossing network.

#### (2) Essential Fire-Support Tasks

| Task | Phase | Action and target | Purpose | Effect / measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B-3F-EFST 1** | I | Suppress and obscure Red forces defending the assigned crossings; conduct counterfire | Enable seizure of OBJ South, Dalen/Central Link Bridge, and OBJ North/North Pass Bridge | Blue controls the crossing sites and PP South is trafficable |
| **B-3F-EFST 2** | II | Suppress Red forces and artillery threatening PP South and the passage lane | Enable the 6th Division to pass without loss of momentum | Lead force crosses BHL, artillery coverage remains continuous, and designated rear element clears PP South |
| **B-3F-EFST 3** | II-IV | Disrupt Red formations massing against an assigned crossing or the division seam | Preserve the crossing network and corps line of communication | Red cannot assemble an unopposed crossing attack; defending brigade retains freedom of action |
| **B-3F-EFST 4** | II-IV | Provide reinforcing or cross-boundary fires on corps order | Support the 6th Division main effort without uncovering PP South | Approved target receives fires while immediate crossing-defense requirements remain supportable |
| **B-3F-EFST 5** | V | Suppress Red forces threatening the 63rd Brigade and obscure withdrawal as directed | Preserve the northern guard force and enable disengagement | Guard clears BHL without being fixed or cut off |
| **B-3F-EFST 6** | V | Transfer fires from the disengaging guard to the crossing defense | Prevent a gap in responsibility after handover | Receiving brigade acknowledges responsibility and engages the following Red force while PP South remains open |

#### (3) Target Priorities

1. Red artillery engaging PP South, a crossing, or a guard-withdrawal route.
2. Armored or mechanized forces able to reach OBJ South or cut the passage lane.
3. Formations massing against North Pass Bridge, Dalen, or Central Link Bridge.
4. Forces threatening the 63rd Brigade's disengagement, BHL, or division seam.
5. Command and sustainment nodes whose disruption directly supports a current essential task.

### d. Scheme of Movement and Survivability

- Occupy setup-designated firing positions that cover OBJ South and the crossing network while retaining lateral and westward displacement routes.
- Coordinate all movement with division passage control; artillery will not enter or cross PP South outside an approved window.
- During the forward passage, 3rd Division Artillery remains positioned to protect PP South while 6th Division Artillery displaces by echelon.
- During Phase V, displace only in coordination with reception and withdrawal traffic. Do not create opposing flow through PP South.
- Displace before counterfire threat, range loss, or route closure prevents the current or next essential task.
- Narrative firing positions do not split the aggregate Atlatl counter.

### e. Tasks to Organic Capabilities

- Maintain target acquisition on Red artillery, crossing threats, Saren bypass routes, PP South, and Phase V pursuit forces.
- Maintain liaison with division headquarters and the 31st, 32nd, and 33rd Brigades.
- Exchange liaison, targets, boundaries, communications, and displacement information with 6th Division Artillery before passage and before Phase V handover.
- Prepare primary and alternate firing areas supporting the approved overlay and passage plan.
- Preserve ammunition for passage protection, threatened crossing defense, and Phase V reception.
- Maintain local security, air-defense awareness, communications, maintenance, recovery, and casualty care internally.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Passage Fire-Support Coordination

Before passage, confirm fire-support boundaries, planned targets, clearance authority, communications, artillery order of march, coverage handoff, and actions if PP South closes. During passage, 3rd Division Artillery protects the passage point and western approach while 6th Division Artillery establishes coverage east of the BHL by echelon. Report the start and completion of each coverage handoff. No handoff is complete until the receiving group confirms communications, range, and target responsibility.

#### (2) Fire-Support Triggers

- Mass on OBJ South when the 31st Brigade begins the decisive crossing seizure or Red can prevent it.
- Shift within the crossing network when a Red force can attack an assigned crossing before the defending brigade can defeat it.
- Execute reinforcing or cross-boundary fires only on authenticated corps or division order and when PP South remains supportable.
- Begin Phase V guard support when the 63rd Brigade enters the northern/central handover area or reports disengagement fires are required.
- Transfer to the crossing defense only after guard clearance, receiving-division acknowledgment, and positive friendly-location confirmation.

#### (3) Clearance and Constraints

- Confirm friendly locations before fires near PP South, BHL, division seams, populated areas, or intermingled passing, guard, and receiving formations.
- Do not abandon immediate crossing-defense fires for a cross-boundary target.
- Do not permit artillery traffic to obstruct the passage lane or guard withdrawal.
- Preserve bridges and critical infrastructure unless destruction is authorized by corps.
- Do not assume effects or ammunition expenditure beyond Atlatl mechanics and agreed adjudication.

#### (4) CCIR

**PIR:** Red formation oriented on PP South or an assigned crossing; location and activity of Red artillery; 5th Guards Armored Brigade movement; Saren bypass indicators; threats to the passage; Red main body's approach to the northern guard sector.

**FFIR:** Group below 70 percent nominal combat power; inability to support PP South, a threatened crossing, passage coverage, or Phase V reception; loss of communications with the priority formation or 6th Division Artillery during handoff; ammunition or mobility insufficient for the next essential task; firing position compromised; displacement route blocked; passage conflict delaying coverage; friendly locations uncertain near a requested target.

#### (5) Reports

Report each turn: current priority formation or area; supported essential task; range and coverage; strength and readiness; ammunition; firing and alternate position; passage or displacement status; and ability to support the current and next phase. During passage, report coverage handoff, lead element at BHL, artillery echelon status, and designated rear element clear. During Phase V, report guard support, BHL clearance, and transfer of fires. Report all FFIR immediately.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept

Sustainment preserves continuous fires across the crossing network while preventing congestion at PP South. Ammunition and fuel movement follow division movement control, remain west of the crossing when practical, and support passage and reception without competing with the 6th Division's decisive movement windows.

### b. Priorities

1. Phase I crossing seizure and counterfire, especially OBJ South.
2. PP South and passage protection.
3. The assigned crossing under immediate threat.
4. Phase V guard disengagement and battle handover.
5. Approved cross-boundary support to the 6th Division.
6. Fuel, maintenance, recovery, communications, and medical support needed to preserve aggregate fires capability.

### c. Transportation, Maintenance, and Recovery

Coordinate artillery traffic with division passage control. No movement creates opposing traffic through PP South during passage or guard withdrawal. Repair priority goes to systems preserving range, communications, mobility, and aggregate fires capability. Recover disabled systems when doing so does not block a crossing or passage route.

### d. Medical and Abstraction

Casualties evacuate west through the division support area. Detailed ammunition handling, survey, target acquisition, maintenance, medical, air-defense, and recovery functions remain incorporated in the artillery counter and are adjudicated narratively.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains in general support under 3rd Compact Armored Division control. The artillery command post occupies a setup-designated protected position that supports centralized control of the crossing network and liaison at PP South. Succession is group commander, deputy commander, then senior organic fires commander. Liaison remains with division and all maneuver brigades; continuous liaison with 6th Division Artillery is required during passage and Phase V handover.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use division fires and command networks. Alternate communications use WCO fires networks and liaison. Contingency communications use relay, prearranged schedules, and messenger. Crossing threats, passage status, coverage handoffs, guard disengagement, battle handover, priority shifts, and FFIR have precedence. Under degraded communications, protect PP South and the last confirmed priority crossing; do not fire near uncertain friendly positions.

## Acknowledgment

Organic leaders acknowledge and backbrief fire priorities, essential tasks, passage coverage, cross-boundary authority, displacement criteria, Phase V transfer conditions, protected infrastructure, and degraded-communications procedures.
