### Task Organization

The group is represented as one aggregate artillery formation with an initial Atlatl strength of 100. Organic command, target acquisition, survey, communications, local security, air defense, sustainment, maintenance, recovery, and medical functions remain incorporated in the counter. No attachments or detachments are in effect at scenario start.

## 1. Situation

### a. Area of Interest

The area of interest extends from AA Axe and PP South through Kasar, Ruda, and Selin to the western Karsovian approaches. It includes the Saren Heights, Mora Hills, southeastern marsh, Red artillery and reserve assembly areas, the Kasar-Ruda-Selin route network, Guard Line Steel, PL Bronze, all routes back to the Arven crossings, and the 1st and 3rd Divisions' Phase V battle-handover areas.

### b. Area of Operations

#### (1) Terrain

The Arven River and PP South form the division's entry and principal line of communication. The crossing and its approaches are vulnerable to congestion, counterfire, and interdiction. East of the river, open basin terrain favors long-range observation and rapid mechanized movement but exposes artillery to acquisition and counterfire. Kasar, Ruda, and Selin are connected by roads that support displacement and ammunition movement while canalizing traffic.

The Saren Heights threaten the northern flank. Marsh southeast of Ruda restricts heavy movement and channels the Selin approach. PL Gold and PL Silver limit maneuver in Phases II and III; PL Bronze divides Phase V guard sectors but is not a movement corridor. The group must displace by echelon to retain range without allowing a single route, bridge, or Red action to trap it east of the corps defense. As one aggregate Atlatl formation, it cannot split into independently playable firing units or provide full simultaneous support to separated fights.

#### (2) Weather and Light

Plan for early-autumn conditions with cool mornings, generally trafficable ground, variable cloud cover, and possible fog or short rain periods. Darkness occurs during each night turn. Fog, rain, smoke, and darkness may degrade target acquisition; wet conditions may increase dependence on the road network and slow ammunition movement or displacement. Weather effects not represented in Atlatl require consistent scenario-control adjudication.

### c. Enemy Forces

The Karsovian Eastern Army controls the 4th and 9th Maneuver Corps and the 5th Guards Armored Brigade reserve. Red can defend Kasar, Ruda, and Selin, reinforce between them, mass artillery against the division main effort or PP South, and counterattack an exposed flank or line of communication. Its artillery can disrupt passage, objective attacks, rear-security handovers, and the Phase V guard withdrawal.

High-value threats are Red artillery supporting the current objective defense or counterattack; armored and mechanized reserves moving from the next objective; forces threatening the northern flank, PP South, or the division line of communication; and the Red main body entering either Phase V guard sector. Red's most dangerous course is to fix the lead brigade at an objective while the 5th Guards Armored Brigade and adjacent corps forces strike the artillery or route to PP South, isolating the division east of the Arven.

### d. Friendly Forces

The 6th Compact Mechanized Division follows and supports the 3rd Compact Armored Division in Phase I and becomes the corps main effort in Phases II-V. The 61st Coalition Armored Brigade leads the Phase II passage and destroys Red forces at Kasar. The 62nd Coalition Mechanized Brigade destroys Red forces at Ruda in Phase III. The 63rd Coalition Mechanized Brigade destroys Red forces at Selin in Phase IV and guards north of PL Bronze as the Phase V main effort. The 62nd Brigade guards south of PL Bronze in Phase V. The 61st Brigade is the Phase V division reserve.

The 3rd Division controls PP South during passage, retains the bridge and western approach, and receives the northern and central guard fight in Phase V. The 1st Ordan Mechanized Division receives the southern guard fight. The 3rd Division Artillery Group protects PP South and may provide reinforcing fires on corps order. The 1st and 3rd Artillery Groups support the guard's disengagement in their receiving sectors and assume primary responsibility for the Red force after battle handover.

### e. Civil Considerations

Kasar, Ruda, Selin, bridge approaches, and connecting routes contain civilians and critical infrastructure. These cities are enemy-force objectives, not enduring terrain-retention missions. Fires require positive identification and proportional effects appropriate to the scenario. Preserve bridges and essential transportation, medical, and civil infrastructure unless destruction is authorized to prevent a greater operational loss.

### f. Assumptions

- No separate observer, radar, air, ammunition, or sustainment counters are created.
- Exact firing areas, range, target eligibility, ammunition effects, and combat results follow Atlatl and scenario-control adjudication.
- The division cannot begin a new objective fight until artillery can support the current objective, next displacement, and rear-security requirements.
- The 1st and 3rd Artillery Groups will establish liaison and reception fires before Phase V battle handover.
- Cross-boundary or reinforcing fires do not change any artillery group's command relationship.

## 2. Mission

Beginning 12 September 2031 at 0600 local, the **6th Division Artillery Group provides general support to the 6th Compact Mechanized Division, protects the forward passage at PP South, isolates and suppresses Red forces sequentially at Kasar, Ruda, and Selin, conducts counterfire, disrupts the Phase V counterattack east of Guard Line Steel, and supports guard disengagement and battle handover in order to enable destruction of the designated Red forces while preserving the division's freedom to withdraw and continue operations.**

## 3. Execution

### a. Commander's Intent

**Purpose:** Maintain decisive, mobile fires with the 6th Division from passage through guard disengagement without allowing the artillery group to become fixed east of the Arven.

**Key tasks:** Coordinate continuous passage coverage; mass on each phase's main-effort brigade; isolate Kasar, Ruda, and Selin; conduct counterfire; interdict reinforcement from the next objective; protect the northern flank; preserve Phase V ammunition; support the 63rd Brigade north and 62nd Brigade south of PL Bronze; keep the 61st Brigade supportable as division reserve; echelon west before routes close; transfer fires without a gap at battle handover.

**End state:** Designated Red forces at Kasar, Ruda, and Selin are destroyed or rendered combat ineffective; the division guard provides warning and delay; both guard brigades disengage in order; the 61st Brigade remains available or is committed effectively; the fight transfers to the 1st and 3rd Divisions; and the artillery group survives west of the handover area with useful combat power.

### b. Concept of Operations

#### (1) Phase I - Follow, Support, and Prepare Passage

Follow and support the division from AA Axe. Coordinate with 3rd Division Artillery on passage targets, boundaries, communications, clearance, artillery sequence, and continuous coverage. Preserve the group and prepare to displace through PP South by echelon without blocking the 61st Brigade's lead movement or the 63rd Brigade's reserve responsiveness. This phase ends when PP South is open, passage control is established, the artillery sequence is confirmed, and the division is in the approved order of march.

#### (2) Phase II - Destroy Red Forces at Kasar

Provide general support with priority to the 61st, then 62nd, then 63rd Brigade. Protect the forward passage, isolate Kasar, suppress defenders, conduct counterfire, and disrupt Red reinforcement or counterattack. Establish coverage east of the BHL by echelon while 3rd Division Artillery continues to protect PP South. Preserve the ability to support the 63rd Brigade if the division reserve is committed. This phase ends when the Kasar force is destroyed or rendered combat ineffective, routes are open, the 63rd Brigade assumes temporary security, and the 61st and 62nd Brigades are postured for Ruda.

#### (3) Phase III - Destroy Red Forces at Ruda

Shift priority to the 62nd, then 61st, then 63rd Brigade. Isolate Ruda, interdict reinforcement from Selin, suppress defenders, protect the 61st Brigade's northern-flank action, and conduct counterfire. Displace only as required to maintain range, survivability, and support to the rear. This phase ends when the Ruda force is destroyed or rendered combat ineffective, the rear remains secure, the 61st Brigade can accept corridor security, and the 62nd and 63rd Brigades are postured for Selin.

#### (4) Phase IV - Destroy Red Forces at Selin

Shift priority to the 63rd, then 62nd, then 61st Brigade. Isolate Selin, suppress defenders, protect the northern flank, and conduct counterfire. Support the transfer of Kasar-Ruda corridor security from the 63rd to the 61st Brigade. Preserve ammunition and westward mobility for Phase V; begin preparation of successive westward firing positions and handover targets. This phase ends when the Selin force is destroyed or rendered combat ineffective, Guard Line Steel and PL Bronze are effective, routes and guard sectors are ready, and receiving divisions can accept the fight.

#### (5) Phase V - Guard, Disengage, and Transfer the Fight

Initial priority is 63rd, then 62nd, then 61st Brigade, shifting to the brigade in contact or disengaging. Disrupt Red forces east of Guard Line Steel, suppress forces threatening either withdrawal, obscure designated routes when directed, and protect the battle-handover areas. The 63rd Brigade guards north of PL Bronze as main effort; the 62nd Brigade guards south; the 61st Brigade remains the division reserve and receives priority when committed to block, counterattack, restore contact, protect artillery, secure a route, or extract a guard brigade.

Displace west by echelon before the group depends on a single threatened route. Coordinate the northern/central transfer with 3rd Division Artillery and the southern transfer with 1st Ordan Artillery. Retain support responsibility for a guard element until it clears the BHL, the receiving division acknowledges responsibility, and fires coordination is transferred. This phase continues through battle handover, reconstitution, and scenario end or subsequent order.

### c. Scheme of Fires

#### (1) Priority Hierarchy

1. **Phase I:** Passage preparation and continuous-coverage coordination.
2. **Phase II:** 61st, 62nd, then 63rd Brigade.
3. **Phase III:** 62nd, 61st, then 63rd Brigade.
4. **Phase IV:** 63rd, 62nd, then 61st Brigade.
5. **Phase V initial:** 63rd, 62nd, then 61st Brigade.
6. **Phase V in contact:** The guard brigade in contact or disengaging.
7. **Phase V reserve commitment:** The 61st Brigade when its mission enables continued guard action, protects artillery or a route, or extracts a guard brigade; otherwise the guard brigade at greatest risk retains priority.

The division commander directs priority shifts. The group commander recommends a shift or displacement whenever enemy action, supported-formation capability, range, route access, ammunition, or survivability threatens the current or next essential task. Priorities do not authorize splitting the aggregate artillery counter.

#### (2) Essential Fire-Support Tasks

| Task | Phase | Action and target | Purpose | Effect / measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B-6F-EFST 1** | I-II | Suppress Red threats to PP South and coordinate continuous passage coverage | Enable the 6th Division to enter the fight without loss of momentum | Lead brigade crosses BHL, communications and fires are established, and artillery coverage remains continuous |
| **B-6F-EFST 2** | II | Isolate Kasar, suppress its defenders, and conduct counterfire | Enable the 61st Brigade to destroy the Kasar force | Red cannot reinforce or withdraw organized combat power; defenders cannot mass against the main effort |
| **B-6F-EFST 3** | III | Isolate Ruda, interdict reinforcement from Selin, and protect the northern flank | Enable the 62nd Brigade to destroy the Ruda force | Red cannot reinforce or withdraw through the Ruda-Selin route; the 62nd Brigade's flank remains protected |
| **B-6F-EFST 4** | IV | Isolate Selin, suppress defenders, and protect the northern flank | Enable the 63rd Brigade to destroy the Selin force | Selin defenders are fixed or disrupted and a northern counterattack cannot defeat the main effort |

| **B-6F-EFST 6** | V | Suppress threats to the 63rd and 62nd Brigades and obscure withdrawal routes as directed | Enable orderly guard disengagement by sector | Guard brigades clear the BHL without being fixed, enveloped, or cut off |

#### (3) Target Priorities

1. Red artillery supporting the current objective defense, counterattack, or guard pursuit.
2. Armored and mechanized reserves able to reinforce the current objective from the next city.
3. Forces threatening PP South, the division line of communication, artillery positions, or withdrawal routes.
4. The Red main body entering the priority guard sector.
5. Forces fixing or enveloping a guard brigade or blocking the 61st Brigade's extraction route.
6. Command and sustainment nodes whose disruption directly supports the current essential task.

### d. Scheme of Movement and Survivability

- Pass PP South by echelon in the division-approved order without blocking the lead brigade, recovery traffic, or the 63rd Brigade's reserve movement.
- Confirm communications, range, and target responsibility before each coverage handoff.
- Maintain at least one viable westward route and report whenever only one route remains.
- Do not displace all artillery capability simultaneously during a main-effort attack, guard contact, or battle handover.
- Begin westward displacement before Red action, route closure, or a phase transition can trap the group east of the receiving defense.
- Do not leave a support node east of a route after the supported maneuver force has withdrawn.
- Narrative echelons and firing positions preserve continuity but do not create additional playable counters.

### e. Tasks to Organic Capabilities

- Maintain target acquisition on Red artillery, objective defenses, reinforcement routes, counterattack forces, guard-sector approaches, and threats to withdrawal routes.
- Maintain liaison with division headquarters and all three maneuver brigades.
- Maintain continuous liaison with 3rd Division Artillery before and during passage.
- Establish liaison with 1st and 3rd Division Artillery and receiving brigades before Phase V.
- Prepare primary, alternate, successive eastward, and successive westward firing areas consistent with the approved overlay and Atlatl movement.
- Protect Phase V ammunition and fuel from expenditure during nonessential targets in earlier phases.
- Maintain local security, air-defense awareness, communications, maintenance, recovery, and casualty care internally.

### f. Coordinating Instructions

#### (1) Forward Passage and Coverage Handoff

Before passage, exchange planned targets, boundaries, clearance procedures, communications, artillery order of march, and actions if PP South closes with 3rd Division Artillery. The 3rd Division group protects PP South and the western approach while this group establishes support east of the BHL by echelon. A coverage handoff is complete only when the receiving command post confirms communications, range, friendly locations, and target responsibility. If PP South becomes unusable, halt short, preserve the group, and await a corps-approved alternate-crossing plan.

#### (2) Objective-to-Objective Transitions

Before shifting priority to the next objective, confirm the previous enemy force is destroyed or contained, the rear-security handover is complete, the next main-effort brigade is postured, targets and friendly locations are current, support range is adequate, ammunition and fuel support the next fight, and a westward displacement route remains open. The group continues the current essential task until the division authorizes transition.

#### (3) Phase V Sector and Handover Coordination

- The 63rd Brigade operates north of PL Bronze and the 62nd Brigade south; PL Bronze is not a fire-support boundary unless separately established.
- Coordinate northern and central reception targets with 3rd Division Artillery and southern reception targets with 1st Ordan Artillery.
- Maintain responsibility for the supported guard formation until BHL clearance, receiving-division acknowledgment, positive friendly-location confirmation, and transfer of target responsibility.
- Avoid firing into a receiving division's area without the required coordination during intermingling.
- Normally support the more heavily engaged guard brigade while the less engaged brigade displaces; adjust on division order.

#### (4) Fire-Support Triggers

- Shift to the designated main-effort brigade on each authenticated phase transition.
- Engage Red reinforcement moving from the next objective when it can affect the current main effort before maneuver can defeat it.
- Shift in Phase V to the brigade in contact or disengaging when Red pressure threatens its freedom of maneuver.
- Shift to the 61st Brigade upon authenticated reserve commitment when its task enables extraction, restores contact, protects artillery, or secures a decisive route.
- Begin an artillery echelon's westward displacement when Red can close or interdict its sole route before the next displacement opportunity.
- Transfer fires by sector only after all battle-handover conditions are met.

#### (5) Clearance and Constraints

- Confirm friendly locations before fires near PP South, BHL, PL Bronze, objective areas, division boundaries, or intermingled guard and receiving forces.
- Remain north of PL Gold and PL Silver restrictions as applicable to the supported maneuver plan.
- Do not expend Phase V reserve ammunition on nonessential targets solely to occupy Kasar, Ruda, or Selin.
- Do not fire solely to restore Guard Line Steel after the delay purpose is achieved.
- Preserve bridges and essential infrastructure unless destruction is authorized by higher headquarters.
- Do not assume effects or ammunition expenditure beyond Atlatl mechanics and agreed adjudication.

#### (6) CCIR

**PIR:** Red force and artillery defending the current objective; Red reinforcement from the next objective; location and commitment of the 5th Guards Armored Brigade; threat to PP South or the division line of communication; Red main-body sector and arrival time at Guard Line Steel; bypass or envelopment threat to either guard brigade.

**FFIR:** Group below 70 percent nominal combat power; inability to support the current objective, next phase, guard disengagement, or 61st Brigade reserve mission; loss of communications with the priority brigade or receiving artillery group; ammunition or fuel insufficient for the next essential task; firing position compromised; only westward route blocked or threatened; an artillery echelon at risk of being trapped; friendly locations uncertain near a requested target.

#### (7) Reports

Report each turn: current priority; supported essential task; range and coverage; strength and readiness; ammunition and fuel; current, next, and fallback position; westward-route status; liaison and handoff status; and ability to support the current and next phase. Report objective transition readiness, target responsibility transfers, reserve commitment support, guard contact, BHL clearance, and all FFIR immediately.

## 4. Sustainment

### a. Concept

Sustainment follows the division by echelon while preserving enough ammunition, fuel, recovery, and route capacity for the next objective and the Phase V withdrawal. Support nodes remain mobile and do not stay east of a route after their supported maneuver echelon withdraws.

### b. Priorities

1. Ammunition and fuel required for the current phase main effort.
2. Counterfire and protection of PP South or the division line of communication.
3. Ammunition reserved for the next objective transition.
4. Phase V disruption, obscuration, guard disengagement, and reserve support.
5. Fuel, maintenance, recovery, communications, and medical support required for westward echeloning.

The group commander reports when projected expenditure would prevent the next essential task and recommends restriction, replenishment, displacement, or phase delay.

### c. Transportation, Maintenance, and Recovery

Coordinate movement with division control and the brigade order of march. Passage, main-effort, withdrawal, and recovery traffic take precedence as directed by the division. Keep support vehicles off PP South and withdrawal routes except during assigned movement windows. Repair priority goes to systems preserving range, communications, mobility, and aggregate fires capability. Recover disabled systems when doing so does not delay a main effort or risk trapping the group.

### d. Medical and Abstraction

Casualties evacuate west along the current division line of communication to the corps crossing defense. Detailed ammunition handling, survey, target acquisition, maintenance, medical, air-defense, and recovery functions remain incorporated in the artillery counter and are adjudicated narratively.

## 5. Command and Signal

### a. Command

The group remains in general support under 6th Compact Mechanized Division control. The artillery command post displaces by echelon or through alternate control arrangements to preserve centralized fires during passage and withdrawal. Succession is group commander, deputy commander, then senior organic fires commander. Liaison remains with division and all maneuver brigades; liaison with 3rd Division Artillery is continuous during passage, and liaison with both receiving artillery groups is continuous during Phase V.

### b. Signal

Primary communications use division fires and command networks. Alternate communications use WCO fires networks and liaison. Contingency communications use relay, prearranged schedules, and messenger. Phase transitions, current main effort, passage and coverage handoffs, Red counterattack, reserve commitment, guard disengagement, battle handover, displacement, and FFIR have precedence. Under degraded communications, support the last confirmed main effort or guard element in contact, preserve the westward route, and do not fire near uncertain friendly positions.
