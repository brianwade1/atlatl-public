# Road to War: The Race for the Ordan Basin

> **Fictional scenario:** All states, organizations, units, leaders, and geographic locations in this document are invented for research and educational use.

## Scenario Parameters

| Item | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict | Competing Western Compact and Karsovian interventions in the Republic of Orda |
| Crisis begins | August 2031 |
| Scenario start | 12 September 2031, 0600 local time |
| Duration | 18 turns / 9 days; two 12-hour turns per day |
| Blue | Western Compact–Ordan Joint Land Corps |
| Red | Karsovian Eastern Army |
| Central problem | A meeting engagement and race for the Arven River crossings, rival political centers, and basin transportation network |

## 1. Strategic Background

The Republic of Orda is a small, formally nonaligned state situated between the **Western Compact** and the larger **Karsovian Federation**. Orda's western frontier borders **Lestara**, a Western Compact member, while its eastern frontier opens toward Karsovia. The country's political and economic center lies in the **Ordan Basin**, an agricultural and industrial region bounded by the **Saren Heights** in the north and the **Mora Hills** in the south.

For three decades, Ordan governments have tried to benefit from both neighboring blocs without joining either. Karsovia remains Orda's largest individual trading partner and supplies much of its energy. Western Compact states provide investment, technology, and access to broader export markets. Orda purchases military equipment from both sources, participates in limited exercises with each, and prohibits permanent foreign bases on its territory. This policy of “balanced nonalignment” enjoys broad public support but depends on stable relations between the two blocs and a functioning political consensus within Orda.

The balance is increasingly difficult to sustain. Karsovia considers Orda part of a historically important security belt and views Western access to the basin as a threat. Western districts generally favor closer relations with the Compact, while eastern districts retain stronger ties with Karsovia. Loyalties remain mixed in every major city, but political parties increasingly define themselves by which outside power they consider the greater danger.

The Ordan Basin gives this competition operational significance. **Veyra**, west of the Arven River, is the national capital. **Ruda**, east of the river, is the principal administrative and commercial center of eastern Orda. **Kasar** and **Selin** connect the basin to Karsovia, while roads from Veyra lead west into Lestara. The cities of **Dalen**, **Novar**, and **Eren** sit on the principal crossings of the **Arven River**. Novar is the decisive road-and-rail junction; Dalen controls the northern route; and Eren anchors the southern route. Secondary crossings at the **North Pass Bridge** and **Central Link Bridge** provide alternatives through more restrictive terrain.

Neither outside power needs to occupy all of Orda to determine its political future. Control of the political centers, Novar, and the principal crossings would permit one side to sustain its intervention and deny the other freedom of action. The strategic contest is therefore a race to establish facts on the ground before diplomacy can halt military operations.

## 2. Political Competition and Erosion of Nonalignment, 2026–2030

### 2026–2027: Economic Pressure and Political Realignment

In 2026, the Ordan government approves the **Basin Renewal Initiative**, a ten-year program to modernize roads, railways, telecommunications, and electrical infrastructure. Western lenders finance much of the program, and Lestaran firms receive contracts to modernize the Veyra–Novar rail line and expand road capacity toward the Arven crossings. Karsovia objects that the agreements give Western companies privileged access to infrastructure with military value.

Karsovia responds with selective customs inspections and temporary reductions in energy deliveries. The measures are officially attributed to technical and regulatory problems, but their political purpose is clear. Eastern Ordan manufacturers suffer production delays, unemployment rises around Ruda and Kasar, and opponents accuse the government of sacrificing neutral trade for Western favor.

During 2027, the Western Compact expands limited cooperation in logistics, communications, disaster response, and infrastructure protection. The program includes no defense guarantee or permanent bases, but Karsovian media portray it as the creation of a Western proxy force. Karsovian organizations simultaneously increase support to political parties and media outlets, including the **Ordan National Renewal Party**, which advocates restored ties with Karsovia, limits on Western military cooperation, and greater provincial authority.

### 2028: Institutions Come Under Pressure

The global slowdown of 2028 strikes Orda unevenly. Western-funded construction continues around Veyra, while factories in the eastern basin lose export orders and face higher energy costs. The resulting disparity becomes a central political grievance. Demonstrations in Ruda and Kasar accuse the national government of favoring western districts; counterdemonstrations in Veyra demand an end to Karsovian economic coercion.

In May, a cyberattack disrupts customs, rail scheduling, and portions of the electrical grid. Veyra attributes it to Karsovian intelligence; Karsovia denies involvement. Parliament passes an election-security and foreign-financing law, but provincial officials in Ruda refuse to enforce several provisions. The armed forces remain formally loyal to the constitution, although regional recruiting and disagreement over internal-security missions create informal networks of divided loyalty.

### 2029: Competing Security Relationships

In February 2029, Orda signs a revised **Partnership and Transit Accord** with Karsovia. The agreement guarantees energy supplies, restores preferential access for Ordan agriculture, and permits limited Karsovian military transit during humanitarian emergencies with prior Ordan approval. In June, Orda concludes a separate **Resilience and Security Cooperation Agreement** with the Western Compact. That agreement provides equipment, training, cyber assistance, and access to Lestaran logistics facilities, but it also requires Ordan consent before foreign forces may enter the country.

The government presents the two agreements as proof that balanced nonalignment remains viable. Instead, each bloc focuses on the concessions granted to the other. Karsovia argues that Western communications equipment and advisers could prepare Orda for Compact membership. Compact governments warn that the transit language in the Karsovian accord could be exploited to introduce forces during a manufactured emergency.

Military activity increases on both frontiers. Karsovia improves rail sites east of Kasar and Selin and rehearses rapid mechanized deployment during its annual **Eastern Assurance** exercises. Lestara upgrades roads and airfields and hosts a Compact reception exercise. Political violence inside Orda also becomes more organized: party offices are attacked, rail lines are sabotaged, and police find arms stockpiles near both Ruda and Veyra.

### 2030: The Neutral Center Collapses

A centrist coalition narrowly survives the April 2030 parliamentary election. The National Renewal Party alleges fraud, although international observers conclude that administrative problems did not change the outcome. Disputes over provincial revenue then produce sustained protests in Ruda. Provincial leaders establish a **Council for Constitutional Renewal**, demand early elections and strict neutrality, and begin meeting openly with Karsovian officials.

The Ordan cabinet accepts additional Compact communications equipment, engineering support, and training for the 1st Ordan Mechanized Division. Karsovia suspends portions of its transit accord. By year's end, Veyra sees Western cooperation as necessary to resist coercion, while Ruda regards it as proof that the government has abandoned neutrality. Both outside powers can now move forces toward Orda quickly and have cultivated Ordan partners able to request their assistance.

## 3. The 2031 Constitutional Crisis

### January–June: An Election Becomes a Strategic Contest

Orda's presidential election is scheduled for 24 August 2031. President **Mara Velen**, the incumbent, campaigns on continued nonalignment combined with stronger safeguards against foreign interference. Her principal opponent, National Renewal leader **Anton Sarek**, promises to restore relations with Karsovia, suspend Western military programs, and negotiate a new federal constitution.

Polling shows a nearly even electorate. Western districts favor Velen; eastern districts favor Sarek; Novar and the central basin remain competitive. Cyber intrusions against the voter registry and manipulated recordings of senior officials deepen mistrust. Both campaigns increasingly portray the election as a final decision over national survival.

Military preparations proceed in parallel. Karsovia announces **Eastern Assurance 31**, beginning in early August, with two corps-level headquarters and supporting forces exercising near the Ordan frontier. Lestara schedules **Compact Resolve 31**, a September command-post and mobility exercise designed to rehearse reinforcement of its eastern region. Orda is invited to send observers to both events but declines to participate as a formation.

In June, the cabinet quietly requests Compact contingency planning for evacuation, infrastructure protection, and assistance if foreign forces enter Orda. The planning creates no automatic commitment, but Karsovian intelligence portrays it as a secret intervention agreement.

### July–August: Disputed Election and Dual Authority

**18 July — Violence at Novar.** Rival campaign groups clash near the Novar rail station. Gunfire kills six people and injures dozens. Police arrest members of both factions, but edited video clips depict the response as a government attack on eastern voters. Demonstrations spread along the Novar–Ruda corridor.

**3 August — Military exercises begin.** Karsovia opens Eastern Assurance 31. Armored and mechanized units deploy toward field training areas opposite Kasar and Selin. Logistics units establish fuel points, field hospitals, air defenses, and communications nodes. Although the exercise is announced months in advance, the inclusion of bridging, civil-affairs, and military-police units gives Karsovia the ability to transition rapidly to an intervention.

**12–20 August — Information systems fail.** Cyber incidents disrupt voter checks and local networks while false polling locations circulate online. Veyra and Ruda each accuse the other of foreign coordination.

**24 August — Election day.** Initial returns give Sarek a narrow lead. Delayed ballots from western districts reverse the result, and the National Election Commission announces that Velen has won by less than one percent. Sarek alleges mass fraud and demands a nationwide recount. Velen calls for calm and agrees to judicial review but refuses demands to suspend the certification process.

**25–27 August — Rival mobilization.** Demonstrations fill Veyra, Ruda, and Novar, while armed groups establish checkpoints east of the Arven. Ruda police begin taking orders from the Council for Constitutional Renewal. Several army battalions fail to report or refuse to act against demonstrators.

**28 August — Certification attempt fails.** The Supreme Electoral Chamber orders a partial recount but rejects a new election. A boycott prevents parliament from certifying Velen's victory. Her government remains in office, while Sarek declares that the constitutional process has been captured.

**29–31 August — Competing governments emerge.** Opposition legislators, provincial officials, and defecting officers form an **Emergency National Council** in Ruda and name Sarek interim president. The cabinet remains in Veyra, recognizes Velen as president-elect, and calls the Ruda action an attempted coup.

Neither authority controls the entire state. Veyra retains the treasury, diplomatic missions, and parts of the security services; Ruda controls much of the eastern basin and several military units. Officials at Dalen, Novar, and Eren avoid choosing sides.

## 4. External Intervention, 1–11 September 2031

### 1–4 September: Requests for Assistance

On 1 September, the Veyra government requests Western Compact assistance to protect constitutional institutions, critical infrastructure, and the national capital. It asks for communications support, air defense, logistics, and ground forces sufficient to deter an advance from Ruda or Karsovia. The request emphasizes that Orda has not joined the Compact and that foreign troops will remain under agreed limitations.

The Ruda council simultaneously asks Karsovia to guarantee public order and prevent what it calls a Western-backed seizure of power. Karsovia recognizes the council on 2 September; the Compact continues to recognize Veyra.

Mediation immediately confronts incompatible positions. Veyra accepts a broader recount but insists that armed groups disarm; Ruda demands a new election and suspension of Western security agreements. Karsovia demands recognition of Ruda's authority over eastern Orda, while the Compact opposes partition.

Meanwhile, the 1st Ordan Mechanized Division begins to fragment. Some elements remain loyal to Veyra, others declare for Ruda, and several local commanders attempt to remain neutral. Equipment is abandoned or disabled during hurried withdrawals. The surviving loyal brigade headquarters pull their playable formations out of the cities and into western assembly areas to reorganize. Municipal police and protective services remain at government facilities, but these local elements are below the scenario's playable scale.

### 5–8 September: The Window for a Peaceful Settlement Narrows

**5 September — Compact authorization.** After emergency consultations, the Western Compact authorizes a limited stabilization mission. The mandate permits forces to enter Orda at the request of the recognized Veyra government, protect civilians and constitutional institutions, secure critical transportation infrastructure, and prevent forcible partition. It does not authorize offensive operations into Karsovia.

The **Western Compact–Ordan Joint Land Corps** forms to control arriving Compact formations and loyal Ordan units. The 3rd Compact Armored Division and 6th Compact Mechanized Division deploy toward the border, while the 7th Coalition Armored Brigade becomes the corps mobile reserve. Political approvals and congested transport networks slow the movement.

**6 September — Karsovian counterdecision.** Karsovia announces that it has accepted the Ruda council's invitation for security assistance. The **Karsovian Eastern Army** receives authority to protect Ruda, secure humanitarian corridors, and prevent Western forces from imposing a government on Orda. Units participating in Eastern Assurance 31 cease exercise play and move to intervention assembly areas without returning to garrison.

**7 September — Breakdown at Novar.** Armed groups exchange fire near the Novar transportation-control center. The local police withdraw, rail traffic stops, and civilian vehicles clog the approaches to the central crossing. Neither Veyra nor Ruda can establish durable control. The disruption severs the fastest east–west route through the basin and makes possession of the surrounding road network a military priority.

**8 September — Arven crossings become local strongpoints.** Municipal police, transport officials, and locally organized civil-defense teams establish temporary checkpoints on the approaches to Dalen, Novar, and Eren. They permit humanitarian traffic but turn away uncoordinated armed groups when they can. None of these detachments is a Blue or Red playable formation, and no intervention brigade occupies a city. The Central Link Bridge and North Pass Bridge remain open but lightly controlled. Both governments accuse the other of preparing to demolish crossings, although no demolition order is confirmed.

### 9–11 September: The Race Begins

**9 September — Diplomacy stalls.** International mediators propose a ceasefire, withdrawal of armed groups from public buildings, joint security of the Arven crossings, and a supervised recount. Veyra accepts the recount framework but rejects joint control with forces loyal to Ruda. Ruda accepts joint control in principle but demands that Velen transfer executive authority before talks continue. Neither side trusts the proposed monitoring force to deploy before foreign troops arrive.

**10 September — Intervention forces cross into Orda.** Western Compact reconnaissance and logistics elements enter from Lestara with Veyra's authorization. Karsovian advance detachments cross the eastern frontier and move toward Kasar, Ruda, and Selin. Both governments describe their movements as lawful responses to invitations from Ordan authorities. Neither declares war on the other.

Compact forces assemble west of Veyra without forming a continuous front. Karsovian forces secure routes east of the basin but remain distributed across several corridors. Both sides receive guidance to avoid unnecessary contact, yet neither is ordered to yield key terrain.

**11 September, morning — Political centers harden.** Civilian police and government protective services maintain checkpoints around official buildings in Veyra. The Emergency National Council organizes similar local security around its headquarters in Ruda and incorporates small groups of defecting personnel. These local forces are narrative actors rather than playable counters; neither political center is occupied by a Blue or Red formation. Civilians flee potential combat areas, while demonstrations and roadblocks continue in several cities.

**11 September, afternoon — Final talks fail.** A direct meeting between Compact and Karsovian representatives ends without agreement. The Compact proposes that both intervention forces remain outside the central basin while Ordan parties negotiate. Karsovia refuses unless the Compact withdraws to Lestara and recognizes the Ruda council as a coequal authority. Each side concludes that delay will allow the other to seize Novar and dominate the river line.

**11 September, evening — Operational orders issued.** The Western Compact–Ordan Joint Land Corps is directed to secure Veyra, prevent the isolation of the elected government, and gain control of the central transportation network. The Karsovian Eastern Army is directed to secure Ruda, establish a protected corridor from the eastern frontier, and place the principal Arven crossings under the control of the emergency council. Both headquarters identify Novar as decisive terrain. Both retain mobile armored forces for exploitation or counterattack.

Through the night, lead formations move toward their designated entry areas without entering the basin's cities. Communications outages, displaced civilians, and contradictory Ordan instructions slow movement. Neither side knows whether local authorities at the crossings will cooperate, disperse, or attempt to obstruct military traffic.

## 5. Transition to Play

### 12 September 2031, 0600: Scenario Start

At the start of the scenario, Orda has two competing governments but no settled line of partition. The Veyra administration retains international recognition from the Western Compact and controls much of the national bureaucracy. The Ruda Emergency National Council holds the principal eastern political center and is recognized by Karsovia. Both claim authority over the entire republic.

The Western Compact–Ordan Joint Land Corps enters from the west. Its Compact divisions are combat capable but have not occupied a prepared defense. The understrength loyal Ordan formations also begin in the western entry area after abandoning their fragmented urban cantonments. No Blue playable formation begins in Veyra, Dalen, Novar, Eren, or any other city. Blue must decide whether to defend broadly, concentrate at Novar, secure the flanking routes, or preserve combat power to protect Veyra.

The Karsovian Eastern Army enters along approaches centered on Kasar, Ruda, and Selin. It has a modest advantage in strength and mechanized combat power but must advance to reach the decisive crossings. Red must decide whether to challenge the entire Arven line, concentrate on Novar, or turn Blue's position through the northern approaches while protecting its communications.

At 0600:

- Veyra and Ruda remain the seats of rival Ordan authorities, but political allegiance does not confer initial military control.
- Veyra, Dalen, Novar, Eren, Kasar, Ruda, and Selin are all neutral and unoccupied by playable units.
- Dalen, Novar, and Eren have not been incorporated into either intervention force's operational posture.
- The principal and secondary Arven crossings remain intact.
- Loyal Ordan formations are understrength, concentrated in the western entry area, and uncertain of support from local authorities.
- Western Compact formations are moving east from Lestara and can reach the central basin quickly, but concentration on one route will expose others.
- Karsovian formations are moving west from multiple frontier entry points and can threaten several objectives, but premature dispersion could allow Blue to defeat them in detail.
- Neither outside power has declared war on the other, although both have authorized their forces to defend themselves and accomplish incompatible missions.
- International mediation continues, but any ceasefire will be shaped by the terrain and political centers each force controls when it takes effect.

The opening is therefore not a deliberate attack against an established defense. It is a meeting engagement in which both forces seek positional advantage before the other can consolidate. Control of bridges and road junctions will determine where combat power can concentrate. Control of Veyra and Ruda will determine which political authority can claim effective governance. The commitment of each side's armored reserve may determine whether an initial bridgehead becomes a defensible corridor or an operational trap.

Blue must preserve the elected government and prevent a territorial fait accompli without dispersing across every approach. Red must establish the Ruda council as the effective government of eastern and central Orda without outrunning support or dividing its force at the Arven. Both face intense pressure to gain a favorable position without broadening the conflict.

## 6. Matters Intentionally Left Unresolved

The shared road-to-war does not answer several questions that may be treated differently in side-specific intelligence products, control instructions, or scenario injects:

- Whether the delayed western ballots changed the legitimate outcome of the 24 August election or merely exposed weaknesses in an otherwise valid process.
- Who directed the cyberattacks against Ordan election, communications, and transportation systems.
- Which political faction first organized and armed the groups operating around Novar and Ruda.
- How much control the Veyra government and Ruda council exercise over loyalist, defecting, and locally organized forces acting in their names.
- Whether Karsovia and the Western Compact planned to intervene before the election or merely prepared options for a rapidly developing crisis.
- Whether either outside power will accept a ceasefire that leaves the opposing force across the Arven River.
- How long the Western Compact will remain politically unified if its forces suffer heavy casualties or appear to be fighting for a disputed election result.
- Whether Karsovia seeks temporary leverage, permanent partition, or control of all Orda.
- The reliability of local officials and Ordan military elements at Dalen, Novar, and Eren once opposing intervention forces arrive.

These uncertainties preserve fog of war, allow competing interpretations of legitimacy, and create opportunities for diplomatic, informational, and military injects without changing the scenario's basic operational problem.

## 7. Design Note

The actors and events in this road-to-war are fictional. The scenario draws on general patterns of political polarization, disputed elections, competing invitations for foreign assistance, military exercises used to reduce intervention timelines, fragmented security forces, multinational crisis response, and efforts to establish a territorial fait accompli before diplomacy can intervene.

The narrative is designed to create a balanced operational contest rather than identify an unambiguous aggressor. Both sides possess a legal and political claim offered by an Ordan authority, both enter from prepared assembly areas, and neither controls the decisive terrain when play begins. This preserves the central purpose of **The Race for the Ordan Basin**: forcing commanders to balance tempo, concentration, bridge control, political objectives, lines of communication, and the timing of the mobile reserve during a fluid meeting engagement.
